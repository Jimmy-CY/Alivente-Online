Administration and Personal, stage A: the markup fault, the borrowed
colour, and the queries that printed

These two modules are to be brought onto base's standards exactly like
every other module. That is several rounds. This one does only the parts
that change NOTHING you can see on screen, so the module is sound before
it is redesigned.

It matters because eleven templates have never had a test pass and SEVEN
of them have already been rewritten by this session's sweeps - the label
round, the components, the panel wrap, the action bar, the Cancel removal,
the headings. Those rounds were aimed at the modules that get tested.
These screens were carried along by them.

THREE PAGES HAD CROSSED TAGS, AND HAVE HAD SINCE BEFORE ANY OF THIS

A </div> closing before the </form> it sits inside. Browsers repair that
silently and not identically, which is how a form works in one browser and
misbehaves in another with nothing in the markup to explain it. The oldest
backup on disk already shows it, so no round in this sequence caused it.

THE REPAIR WAS READ OFF THE NESTING, NOT GUESSED. user_add's three panels
close at 278, 298 and 352; the </div> at 353 has nothing left to close and
the container opened at 220 is closed by the one at 358 - so that tag is
simply EXTRA. user_edit is the same shape. user_permissions is the
opposite: its .form-card at 331 is never closed, so a </div> is inserted
before the </form>.

Each page's mismatch count must reach ZERO, which is a stronger statement
than "it got better" - and the suite requires zero across all eleven, so
a new one cannot arrive unnoticed.

THE COLOUR, AND THE SURVEY THAT CORRECTED ME WHILE I WROTE IT

#667eea appears 49 times across these screens. I had the survey section
titled "a colour the palette does not contain" - and its own control line
came back saying IT IS IN BASE, CHECK THIS. It was right: base uses that
purple, paired with #764ba2, for the avatar circle with the user's
initials. Deliberate, and untouched here - 11 uses stay.

The other 38 are icons, borders and focus rings borrowing the avatar's
purple where --alv-accent belongs. Same fault as the red focus ring on the
Financials expense screens: nobody chose it, it arrived, and no rule
stopped it. A pairing with #764ba2 marks the avatar; everything else takes
the token.

A report that cannot contradict its author is not worth printing.

NINE QUERIES THAT PRINTED

Nine of the eleven carried a max-width query with no `screen` keyword. A4
portrait is about 718 CSS px, so they fired on paper and printed the page
in phone layout. The keyword changes nothing on a screen.

The rest of the system still has 79 such queries on 63 pages. Those are
section 2.I's. These nine are here because this round is about bringing
one module onto the standard, not because the paper problem is solved.

VERIFIED: 11 checks, sabotaged three ways - re-cross a tag, put the purple
back, unguard a query. Section 1 also proves it can SEE a crossed tag,
tested on a string rather than on the repo, so it cannot quietly go vacuous
the day the repo is clean. Section 4 renders user_edit's own stylesheet
under base and reads the focus ring back at rgb(14, 124, 139), with
transitions disabled - read at 0ms it would still be the resting colour,
which is how a focus check passes whatever the rule says.

Every suite run before and after with full output compared: three differ,
two hold their verdicts exactly and the third is this round's own.
