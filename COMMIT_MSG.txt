base declares .form-card and it carries the Customer Invoice wash. On
Properties > Add and Tenants > Add nothing changed, because those pages
HAVE NO PANEL ELEMENT. Their fields sit straight on the page. A rule
cannot paint a box that is not there.

That was the real answer to "why does it not look like Customer Invoice",
and it was never a colour problem. Customer Invoice was one of ten screens
that already had a panel, which is precisely why it was the screen that
looked right.

WHAT THIS ROUND DOES, IN THREE CASES

  WRAP - 14 Add and Edit screens whose fields sat loose get one panel
  around everything in the form AFTER the action bar. That is where the
  model screen puts its own: bar above, panel below.

  RENAME - suppliers_add and suppliers_edit already had a panel and called
  it .form-section. The class becomes .form-card and the page's own rule
  for it goes, because base declares the same six properties. One name for
  one thing.

  REPORT AND LEAVE - cash_receipt_add carries the house .alv-card and
  generate_lease_agreement carries Bootstrap's .card. Both already have a
  box. Putting a panel around a panel is not what was asked for, and
  choosing between two existing components is a decision rather than a
  sweep. Both are named.

24 of the 26 Add and Edit screens are now on the component.

ONE PANEL, NOT THREE, AND THAT IS A DELIBERATE STOP

The model screen has three panels with titles - Customer, Settings, Lines.
Splitting the other screens that way means deciding, per page, which
fields belong together. That is design work and a tool cannot do it. One
panel per screen is mechanical, reversible, and puts the wash on every
Add and Edit screen today. Titled groups can follow per page, by eye, and
the suite says in as many words that it does not check the grouping.

THE FAILURE THAT WOULD REALLY HURT IS INVISIBLE IN THE DIFF

A wrap is a markup change on signed-off pages. The dangerous case is not a
crooked box: it is a panel whose opening tag sits inside an {% if %} and
whose closing tag sits outside it. Then the panel closes only sometimes,
and the page comes apart for one kind of user and not another - in
production, for somebody without a permission I have.

So no page is wrapped until its region is proved self-contained: at least
one control in it, every HTML tag it opens closed inside it, EVERY DJANGO
BLOCK TAG it opens closed inside it, and no nested form. Any page failing
any of those is reported and skipped rather than guessed at. The suite
then re-checks it from the other end, computing the block-tag depth at the
panel's opening tag and at its closing tag and failing if they differ.

WHAT THE SUITE FOUND THAT THIS ROUND DID NOT CAUSE

Five screens that already had a panel put their action bar at the BOTTOM
of the form - customer_form, user_add, user_edit, workspace_add,
workspace_edit - where the model screen puts Save and Back at the top
right. That is REPORTED, not failed. Moving a Save button is visible and
preference-bearing: a decision somebody takes, not a fault a sweep
corrects. It belongs to the action-bar round.

And customer_form puts its panel OUTSIDE its form rather than inside. The
first spelling of that check required the panel to be inside and reported
the page as broken. Either nesting is legitimate; what must never happen
is the two OVERLAPPING, which is what a wrap at the wrong offset would
produce and what no browser recovers from predictably. That is the check
now.

ONE BUG, THE SAME FAMILY AS THE WEEK'S OTHERS

The rename check asked whether the string "form-section" survived.
form-section-title contains it, is a different class, and is one this
round keeps - so both Suppliers pages reported a failed rename and the
patcher refused to write anything at all. It asks for the standalone token
now. Three times this week a check has been pointed at a substring instead
of at the thing it names.

VERIFIED: 13 checks, including the panel rendered in Chromium against
base's real stylesheet - the wash, 20px by 24px, 12px, the softer shadow -
with a plain div as the control. Every suite in the repo was run before and
after with full output compared, not just summary lines: two differ, this
round's own and test_form_components counting two more media-query rules
because Suppliers' renamed phone rule now reads as .form-card. The other
82 are byte-identical.

NOT PROVED HERE: that the fields inside each panel are grouped well. One
box around everything is not the same as a well-organised screen.
