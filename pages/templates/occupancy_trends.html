What is this report?

The Performance Trends page is the multi-year drill-down from Vacancy Management. Where Vacancy Management shows you a snapshot for a selected time period, this page shows you the same three occupancy KPIs plotted across every year your portfolio has been active — from the very first tenancy through to today.

The Back button takes you back to Vacancy Management, not to the main Financials page — reinforcing that this page is a deeper view of the Vacancy Management data, not an independent report.

Date range

The range shown is automatic — the page starts at the year of your first tenancy in any property, and runs through to the current year. You don't configure this; it's driven entirely by the data.

In the subtitle at the top (e.g. "Historical performance metrics from 2023 to 2026"), the starting year is the year of your very first lease start date, and the ending year is always the current year.

Inherits the opt-in rule from Vacancy Management

Like Vacancy Management, only properties flagged with "Include in Occupancy" feed into these trends. Owner-occupied, short-term, and excluded properties don't appear.

No property filter — this is portfolio-level only
Unlike Vacancy Management, there's no Property Selection panel here. The three KPIs are always calculated across your entire tracked portfolio. If you want to see how a specific property compares to the portfolio over time, the year-detail drill-down modal shows per-property breakdowns for any year you click into.
The YTD badge

The current year row in the table (and the last point on each chart) is labelled YTD — "Year to Date". This is important because:

  • The current year isn't complete yet — you're comparing a partial year against fully-completed prior years
  • If the YTD number looks better than prior years, it might just be because the bad months haven't arrived yet. Or it might be genuine improvement — you'll only know for sure at year-end.
  • Trends ending at the YTD point should be read with this caveat in mind
When to use this report
  • Annual portfolio review — has occupancy been trending up, down, or flat? Are we better or worse than three years ago?
  • After a bad year — was 2025's spike in vacancies a one-off or part of a trend?
  • When presenting to investors or partners — multi-year performance is more compelling than a single snapshot
  • Investment decisions — consistently poor vacancy years are a red flag when deciding whether to hold or sell

Reading the Three Line Charts

The top of the page shows three line charts, one per KPI, each covering the full year range. They all share the same layout:

  • X-axis: years (oldest on the left, current year on the right, labelled YTD)
  • Y-axis: the KPI value — percent, days, or euros
  • Main line with markers: the KPI value for each year
  • Red dashed line: the average across all the years shown — the benchmark for comparing individual years
Chart 1 — Occupancy Rate

Teal line with a filled area below. Higher is better. Years where the line is above the red dashed average are better-than-average years. Years below are worse-than-average years.

Chart 2 — Avg Days to Fill

Orange line with a filled area. Lower is better. Interpretation is reversed: years below the red dashed average are your good years (fast to re-let); years above are problem years (slow to re-let).

Chart 3 — Vacancy Cost

Pink line with a filled area. Lower is better. Same reversed interpretation as Days to Fill: below the average = good, above = bad. Spikes are years when vacancy cost you a lot of revenue.

Interacting with the charts
  • Hover a point — a tooltip shows the KPI value and a context figure (property count for Occupancy, vacancy count for the other two). Also prompts "Click for detailed breakdown".
  • Click a point — opens the Year Detail modal for that year, same as clicking the year in the table below
  • Hover the red line — shows the all-years average value
How to read trends
Upward trend on Occupancy Rate, downward trend on Days to Fill & Cost

The portfolio is improving. Whatever you've been doing (pricing, agent selection, property condition) is working. Keep going.

Downward trend on Occupancy, upward trend on Days to Fill & Cost

The portfolio is deteriorating. Investigate. Is it one problem property dragging the average, or a systemic issue? Click into recent years to diagnose.

Flat trend near or above/below the average

Stable performance. This is actually a good outcome — consistent, predictable occupancy.

Single-year spike or dip

One anomalous year surrounded by normal years. Click into that year to understand what happened — was it a specific property or event, or a broader problem that self-corrected?

The Yearly Data Summary table

Below the charts, the same data appears in a row-by-row format with additional raw data not shown on the charts. Every row is clickable and opens that year's detail modal.

The 8 columns
  • Year — the calendar year. Current year carries a blue YTD badge.
  • Occupancy % — same value shown in the Occupancy Rate chart, to one decimal place
  • Avg Days to Fill — same value shown in the Days to Fill chart
  • Vacancy Cost — same value shown in the Vacancy Cost chart, in euros with thousands separators
  • Properties — how many properties were tracked that year. This changes over time as properties are added to or removed from occupancy tracking
  • Vacancies — the count of vacancy events (not vacant days) that started or ended in the year
  • Available Days — the total days the tracked properties could have been rented in the year (roughly: property count × days in year, adjusted for partial-year properties)
  • Occupied Days — the total days the tracked properties were actually rented
The raw data lets you verify the KPIs

The Occupancy % column is literally Occupied Days ÷ Available Days × 100. So if you see 99.0% with 1,375 available days and 1,361 occupied days, you can verify: 1,361 ÷ 1,375 = 98.98%, which rounds to 99.0%. The raw numbers are shown for transparency.

Click any row for detail

Clicking anywhere on a year's row opens the Year Detail modal — the same behaviour as clicking a point on a chart. Hovering a row shows a subtle highlight so you know it's clickable.

The table and the charts show the same KPI values in different formats. The charts are best for spotting trends at a glance; the table is best for looking up exact numbers or investigating the underlying data (property count, vacancy count, raw days).
The Year Detail Modal

Click any year (in the table or on any chart) and a large modal opens with the full breakdown for that year. This is where you investigate why a year looked the way it did.

Period summary

Blue info bar at the top showing: the year (with "Year-to-Date" suffix for the current year), number of properties operational that year, and the number of vacancy periods.

Three metric cards

The same three KPIs shown in the trend charts, but now with additional context below each big number:

  • Occupancy Rate — shows "X / Y days" beneath the percentage, making the underlying math visible (e.g. "184 / 184 days" means 184 occupied out of 184 available)
  • Avg Days to Fill — shows the vacancy count, so "1.8 days over 3 vacancies" tells a different story than "1.8 days over 20 vacancies"
  • Vacancy Cost — labelled "Lost revenue" to reinforce what the number really represents
Property Performance Breakdown table

One row per property tracked that year, showing:

  • Property name
  • Available Days — days this property could have been rented in the year
  • Occupied Days — days it was actually rented
  • Vacant Days — the difference (Available − Occupied)
  • Occupancy Rate — shown as a colour-coded badge
The badge thresholds on this modal differ slightly from the Vacancy Management Performance Modal.

On this page:

  • Green — Occupancy ≥ 95%
  • Yellow — Occupancy between 85% and 94%
  • Red — Occupancy below 85%

These are wider tolerance bands for reviewing historical full-year data, where seasonal fluctuations naturally produce a broader spread.

Vacancy Periods table

Below the property breakdown, the individual vacancy events that occurred during the year:

  • Property — which property the vacancy happened on
  • Start Date — when the property became vacant
  • End Date — when a new tenant moved in (or the end of the period if still vacant)
  • Days — the length of the vacancy
  • Previous Tenant — the tenant who moved out
  • Next Tenant — the tenant who moved in (if filled)
  • Cost — the revenue lost during this specific gap, calculated from the rent of the next lease (if signed) or the previous lease (if still vacant)

If the year had no vacancies at all, this section shows a green banner: "No vacancies recorded for this period!" — a perfect year.

The Vacancy Periods table is the most actionable information on this page. It lets you pinpoint exactly when a property was vacant, for how long, between which tenants, and at what cost. When investigating a bad year, this is where you go.
Practical Tips
Reading trends properly
  • Trends matter more than single years. A single bad year might just be variance; a three-year deterioration is a pattern worth investigating. Don't panic after one bad year; don't celebrate after one good one.
  • YTD fairness — don't compare apples to oranges. The current year's number is a partial-year figure. Comparing "2026 YTD Vacancy Cost = €0" against "2025 full-year Vacancy Cost = €11,162" isn't meaningful in April 2026 — you haven't had time to accumulate vacancies yet.
  • The red dashed line is your benchmark. Years above the average on Occupancy are wins; years above the average on Days-to-Fill or Cost are problems. Memorise this — the interpretation reverses depending on the metric.
  • Look for regime changes. Did something change in a specific year? A new letting agent, a major renovation, a market downturn? The charts help you spot when things turned, and the Year Detail modal helps you diagnose why.
Investigating a specific year
  • Start with the metric cards. Before anything else, check the three metric cards to get the headline numbers. If "1.8 days over 3 vacancies" vs "1.8 days over 20 vacancies" — the second year is clearly worse even though the average is the same.
  • Property Performance Breakdown shows you the culprits. If one year had 90% portfolio occupancy but one property dragged it down to 61%, you'll see it immediately on the breakdown. That's where to focus.
  • Vacancy Periods reveals the individual events. A long single gap (e.g. 180 days) is a very different problem from ten short gaps (e.g. 18 days each). Long gaps = slow re-letting; short gaps = high churn. Different solutions.
  • Previous/Next Tenant columns can reveal patterns. Is the same tenant leaving repeatedly (implies short leases, maybe corporate tenants)? Is there always a gap before the same returning tenant (implies seasonal arrangement)? The names tell a story the numbers don't.
Using the page for decisions
  • Investor reports. A screenshot of the three trend charts is often the clearest way to communicate portfolio health to co-owners, family members, or financial advisors.
  • Property sale/hold decisions. A property that appears in multiple Year Detail modals as the main culprit — across different years — is a chronic problem, not a recent issue. That's the one you might consider selling.
  • Agent evaluation. If Days to Fill is trending up while nothing else has changed, the letting agent might be slowing down. If Days to Fill dropped dramatically in a specific year, that's worth understanding — did you change agent, or change something about pricing/presentation?
  • Plan improvements. The years with the highest Vacancy Cost on this page are the ones you don't want to repeat. What was different in your best years? Can you replicate those conditions?
Connecting back
  • The Back button returns to Vacancy Management. Use it when you've understood the trends and want to drill into a specific property's current-state metrics.
  • Vacancy Management for current state, Performance Trends for history. The two pages complement each other — Vacancy Management answers "how are we doing now?"; Performance Trends answers "how have we been doing over time?" Use both for a complete view.
  • The property count can reveal portfolio growth. If 2023 shows 1 property and 2026 shows 9 properties, that's the rate at which you've been adding properties to occupancy tracking. Growth itself can distort averages — a new addition with an opening-year vacancy can drag the whole portfolio's numbers down.
This page is about pattern recognition. Individual year metrics are on Vacancy Management; the story those numbers tell — the improvement, the decline, the turning points, the chronic problems — lives here.
