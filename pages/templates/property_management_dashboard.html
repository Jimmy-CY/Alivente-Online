Property Dashboard — User Guide

An immersive, visual hub for exploring everything about a property
×

What is the Dashboard?

The Property Dashboard is a visual, interactive hub for exploring everything about a single property. Instead of menus and tables, you get a glassmorphic radial interface: a centre property selector surrounded by 8 main modules, several of which expand into sub-modules.

Pick a property in the centre, then click whichever angle of the property you want to explore — legal documents, financial performance, tenants, expenses, issues, valuation, and so on.

What's on screen

  • Centre hub — the property selector. Starts as "Select Property"; once you've picked one, it shows the property name.
  • 8 main module cards arranged in a circle around the hub.
  • 5 sub-module cards branching off the Tenant and Expenses cards.
  • Connection lines linking the hub to modules and modules to sub-modules — they brighten when a property is selected.
Cards are dim until you select a property. That's intentional — nothing to click on until the system knows which property you want to look at.

Three steps to use the Dashboard

1. Click the centre "Select Property" hub

A modal opens listing all your properties.

2. Pick a property from the list

The modal closes, the centre hub shows your property name, and every card around it lights up — the dashboard is now "armed" for that property.

3. Click any module card

You're navigated to the relevant detail view for the selected property — e.g. its title deed, its tenants, its open invoices, its profit & loss summary.

Switching property

Click the centre hub at any time to swap to a different property. All cards reflect the new selection immediately.

Deep linking

You can pre-select a property by adding ?property=ID to the dashboard URL. Useful when linking from other parts of the system.

Keyboard

Esc closes the property picker (or this Help modal).

The eight main module cards

Each card opens a detail view scoped to the selected property:

Title Deed

View-only. Displays the uploaded title deed PDF for the property in an inline viewer. To upload, replace or delete the deed itself, use the Properties module.

Property Report

A multi-section snapshot of the property: Property Address, Specifications (square meters, year built), Utility Accounts, Insurance & Status, plus an Asset Summary covering asset statistics, financial summary (purchase value, maintenance cost), assets grouped by category, and a full Asset Details table with warranty expiry dates.

Tenant

The current tenant for the property: Type (Individual or Company), Contact Information, Lease Period (start/end dates), Financial Information (deposit, monthly rent, levies, payment terms), and Lease Details (rental type, renewal clause, notice period, renewal status, active flag). Branches into Lease, Invoices and Lease Renewals sub-modules.

Issues

Maintenance, repairs and any open issues raised against this property. When there are none, displays a green "Excellent! No issues have been reported" empty state.

Valuation

Compares the original Purchase Price against the Current Value, with the gain or loss shown in € and as a percentage, accompanied by a green/red trend arrow.

Revenues

A breakdown of the property's revenue lines — typically Rental and Levies — each with its Revenue Type (Monthly, Yearly) and Amount. Pulled from the tenant's lease data.

Expenses (parent only — not directly clickable)

The Expenses card itself is intentionally not clickable. You'll get a notification asking you to pick either Actual or Budgeted instead. Those are the two sub-module cards branching off it.

Profit & Loss

A full 12-month P&L statement for the property: Revenue rows (Levies, Rental), Expenses rows (Communal Fees, Company Tax, Insurance, Prop Tax, Property Manager, etc.) and a Net Profit row — broken down month by month with additional Total and Monthly average columns. A Budget toggle in the top-right switches between Actual and Budget views. Below the table, a Financial Indicators panel surfaces headline KPIs: Gross ROI, Net ROI, Expenses to Revenue, Rent €/m², and % Value Increase.

Sub-module cards

Five smaller cards branch off two of the main modules:

Branching from Tenant

Lease

View-only. Inline PDF display of the current lease agreement document. Page title shows the property and tenant in parentheses for context.

Invoices

Open invoices for this property's current tenant. The header shows the Total Amount Due in red. Each row lists Invoice Date, Due Date, Days Overdue, Amount, and a red Overdue badge in the Status column. Scoped to this property only — for the system-wide list, use the main Invoices module.

Lease Renewals

The renewal state of the current lease. While the lease is still active, displays a green confirmation: "Current lease is active. No renewal action required at this time." together with the tenant name and lease end date. As expiry approaches, the screen prompts for renewal action.

Branching from Expenses

Actual

A table of expenses actually incurred for this property, with columns for Date, Description, Amount, and a green invoice icon that opens the scanned receipt PDF in a popup viewer. A Year filter sits in the top-right; the bottom row sums to Total Actual Expenses for [year].

Budgeted

The planned expense schedule for this property. Each row lists an Expense Line Type (Communal Fees, Company Tax, Insurance, Property Manager, Refuse, etc.), an Expense Type indicating when the cost falls (a specific month like June for one-off payments, or Monthly for recurring), and the budgeted Amount. This is the baseline that Actual is compared against.

Why split Expenses into Actual and Budgeted? Comparing the two is the heart of expense control. Looking at one without the other only tells half the story.

Best Practices

  • Always pick a property first — everything else is locked until you do. The dim cards are a deliberate hint.
  • Use this as your starting point per property — if you're investigating one specific property end-to-end, the Dashboard is faster than navigating module-by-module from the top menu.
  • Don't try to click the Expenses parent card — it's deliberately a "container" only. Click Actual or Budgeted instead.
  • Compare Actual vs Budgeted — click both in turn to spot variances. Big swings either way are worth investigating.
  • Switch properties without leaving the page — click the centre hub again any time. Faster than going Back and re-entering.
  • Use deep links — bookmark ?property=ID URLs for properties you check often.
  • Profit & Loss is the executive view — the full 12-month grid plus the Financial Indicators row gives you a property's complete financial picture in one screen. If you only have time for one card per property, it's this one.
  • Use the Budget toggle on Profit & Loss — flip between Actual and Budget views to see where the property is over- or under-performing against the plan.
Cards still dim after picking a property? Try clicking the centre hub and re-selecting. If the issue persists, refresh the page.
