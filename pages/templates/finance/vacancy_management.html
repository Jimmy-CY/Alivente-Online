What is this report?

The Vacancy Management dashboard tracks how well your properties stay rented over time. Where the Financial Indicators dashboard shows you income performance, this one shows you the occupancy side of the story — which complements rather than duplicates income analysis.

Three occupancy KPIs are calculated for each property and benchmarked against the portfolio average:

  • Occupancy Rate — the percentage of time a property has a paying tenant
  • Avg Days to Fill — how long it takes to re-let a property between tenants
  • Vacancy Cost — the revenue lost during empty periods
Opt-in — only "rental" properties appear
Not every property in your portfolio is shown on this page.

Only properties with the "Include in Occupancy" flag ticked appear here. This is set per-property on the Properties module. The point of the flag is to exclude properties where traditional occupancy benchmarks don't apply:

  • Owner-occupied properties — where you or your family live; no rental = no vacancy metric
  • Short-term / summer-let properties — where the let pattern is seasonal and comparing "% of the year occupied" against traditional long-term rentals would be misleading
  • Properties not yet rented or no longer rented — properties between investment phases

If you have 10 properties in the portfolio but only 9 appear on this page, it means 1 is flagged as excluded from occupancy tracking. Toggle the flag on the Properties module if you want it included.

Four layers of visualisation

Like the Financial Indicators page, the dashboard flows top-to-bottom in a natural reading order:

  1. Time Period selector — scope the analysis to the past year, past 3 years, or all time
  2. Consolidated Occupancy Indicators cards — clickable for drill-down
  3. Property Selection panel — filter which of the tracked properties are included
  4. Three mini bar charts — one per KPI, sorted best-to-worst
  5. Detailed Property Data table — sortable columns with Portfolio Average row pinned
  6. Historical Performance Trends button — jump to multi-year occupancy charts
When to use this report
  • After a tenant moves out — monitor how quickly that property re-lets and whether the vacancy is dragging
  • Annual review — which properties had the best and worst occupancy this year? Are they the same ones that had issues last year?
  • Deciding whether to change letting agent or pricing — a property with chronically low occupancy often signals a pricing or marketing problem
  • Pre-purchase diligence — historical occupancy patterns help identify properties that have been consistently easy or difficult to keep let

The Three Occupancy Indicators

Each indicator captures a different dimension of occupancy performance. Read all three together for a complete picture.

1. Occupancy Rate (%)

Formula: (Days Occupied ÷ Total Days in Period) × 100

What it tells you: What percentage of the selected time period a property had a paying tenant. A property occupied for 330 out of 365 days in the past year has a 90.4% Occupancy Rate.

Higher is better. The industry benchmark for healthy long-term residential rentals is usually 95% or above.

2. Avg Days to Fill

Formula: Average number of days between one tenant moving out and the next tenant moving in, across all turnover events in the period.

What it tells you: How quickly a property re-lets when it becomes vacant. A property with an Avg Days to Fill of 15 days is turning around quickly; one with 60+ days is taking a long time (and costing you revenue).

Lower is better.

Why some properties show "0 days". A zero value doesn't mean a property filled instantly — it means there were no vacancy gaps to measure in the selected period. This can happen for three reasons:
  • The tenant renewed their lease — no gap, no measurement needed
  • Back-to-back tenants — the new tenant moved in the same day the previous one moved out (or the next day)
  • No turnover at all in the period — the same tenant has been there the whole time

In all three cases, 0 days is a good thing — shown as Excellent in the Performance Modal.

3. Vacancy Cost (€)

Formula: For each vacant day: Days Vacant × (Monthly Rent ÷ 30)

What it tells you: The actual revenue you didn't earn because of vacant periods. If a property rents for €900/month and sits vacant for 30 days, the Vacancy Cost is ~€900. If the same property sat vacant for 120 days, it would be ~€3,600 in lost revenue.

Which rent is used for the calculation? The system is intelligent about this:

  • If a new tenant has already been signed (but hasn't moved in yet), the next lease's rent is used — that's the rent you'll be collecting soon
  • If the property is still actively vacant, the previous lease's rent is used — that's the best estimate of what it would be earning if still let

Lower is better. This is the single most meaningful indicator because it translates vacancy into real money lost.

Performance tier thresholds
Excellent

Occupancy Rate: value is 100% — "Perfect" (the property has been occupied for the entire period).
Avg Days to Fill / Vacancy Cost: value is zero (Perfect) or 80% or less of the portfolio average.

Good

Occupancy Rate: 95% to 99.9% — strong but not perfect.
Avg Days to Fill / Vacancy Cost: 80% to 100% of the portfolio average.

Needs Improvement

Occupancy Rate: below 95%.
Avg Days to Fill / Vacancy Cost: more than 100% of the portfolio average.

Occupancy Rate uses absolute thresholds, not relative. Unlike the Financial Indicators page, where tiers are 80%/120% of portfolio average, Occupancy Rate has fixed absolute cutoffs: 100% = Excellent, 95-99.9% = Good, <95% = Needs Improvement. The reason: 95% is a well-established industry benchmark for healthy residential rentals, so measuring against the portfolio's own average could mask a problem (if the whole portfolio is under-occupied, you'd want every property flagged — not just the worst ones).
Reading the Dashboard
Time Period selector

Three buttons at the top scope the entire analysis to a different time window:

  • Past Year — the last 365 days from today. Best for routine monitoring and year-to-date review.
  • Last 3 Years — the last ~1,095 days. Useful for spotting longer-term patterns (a property that looks fine this year may have been problematic last year).
  • All Time — from the property's first recorded tenancy through to today. This gives you the lifetime occupancy picture, useful for identifying properties that have been consistently troublesome.

The display strip underneath shows the actual date range for the selected period (e.g. "Past Year: 19 Apr 2025 - 19 Apr 2026") so you always know exactly what you're looking at.

Consolidated Occupancy Indicators cards

Three clickable cards show the portfolio-level figure for each indicator — Occupancy Rate, Avg Days to Fill, Vacancy Cost — calculated across all properties included in occupancy tracking. Hover for the "Click for details" hint; click to open the Performance Modal for that indicator.

Property Selection panel

Only the properties with the Include in Occupancy flag appear here. Use the checkboxes to filter down further (for example, to focus on just Cyprus properties, or just a single troublesome property). Select All / Select None shortcuts work as usual.

Everything downstream — the mini charts, the detailed table, the Portfolio Average row, and the drill-down modals — recalculates on the reduced scope.

The three mini bar charts

One chart per indicator, displayed in a responsive grid. Each chart has:

  • Portfolio Average shown in the header in the indicator's accent colour
  • Bars sorted best-to-worst from left to right
  • Colour-coded bars: Green (Excellent tier), Yellow (Good tier), Red (Needs Improvement tier)
  • Orange dashed line showing the Portfolio Average benchmark
Detailed Property Data table

Every selected property as a row, each KPI as a column. Features:

  • Sortable columns — click any header to sort by that KPI. The active column is highlighted teal with a directional arrow.
  • Portfolio Average row — pinned at the bottom in teal, showing the consolidated figure
  • Clear units — percentages for Occupancy Rate, "days" for Avg Days to Fill, "€" for Vacancy Cost
Historical Trends button

At the bottom of the page, a prominent CTA links to the Occupancy Trends report — a separate page that shows multi-year occupancy performance charts, letting you see how each KPI has changed over time. Use it when you need to move beyond a single time snapshot and see the direction of travel.

The Property Performance Modal

Clicking any of the three Consolidated Occupancy Indicator cards opens the Property Performance Details modal — the detailed drill-down for that one indicator. The structure matches the Financial Indicators page's modal for consistency.

What the modal shows
  • Header block — KPI name, definition, and Portfolio Average for the selected time period
  • Performance legend — the three colour-coded tiers explained inline
  • Ranked property table with five columns:
    • # — rank position (best first)
    • Property Name
    • Performance — the KPI value for this property
    • vs Portfolio Avg — percentage difference, or "Perfect" for zero-vacancy / 100%-occupancy properties
    • Performance Level — a coloured badge: EXCELLENT / GOOD / NEEDS IMPROVEMENT
  • Summary cards at the bottom — count of properties in each performance tier
How the ranking works
Occupancy Rate is ranked highest first. 100%-occupied properties appear at the top, marked "Perfect".
Avg Days to Fill and Vacancy Cost are ranked lowest first. Properties at zero (no vacancy events) appear at the top, also marked "Perfect". The properties with the worst vacancy problems drop to the bottom.

In all three cases, the property at rank #1 is always your "best performer" for this KPI, and the property at the bottom is the one that most needs attention.

Row colour shading

Each row is subtly tinted based on its performance tier — light green for Excellent, light yellow for Good, light red for Needs Improvement. This makes it easy to see where the tier boundaries fall across the portfolio.

Export to Excel

The Export to Excel button downloads the ranked list as a properly-formatted .xlsx file with filename e.g. VacancyPerformance_occupancyRate_2026-04-19.xlsx. Includes the indicator name, generation date, Portfolio Average, and the full ranked table.

Practical Tips
Using the time period correctly
  • Start with Past Year for routine monitoring. It's the most immediately relevant window — what's been happening lately? A problem property in the past year is something you can act on now.
  • Use Last 3 Years to spot chronic issues. A property that's been in the Needs Improvement tier for three consecutive years has a structural problem, not a recent stroke of bad luck. Time to review the pricing, condition, location, or letting agent.
  • Use All Time for investment analysis. When considering whether to sell or hold a property, lifetime occupancy patterns are a better signal than the last 12 months. A property that's been 95%+ occupied for 5+ years is a strong asset; one that's been in the low 80s is worth examining.
Reading the three KPIs together
  • Vacancy Cost is the one that matters most. It translates occupancy directly into euros. A property with 85% Occupancy Rate might sound fine, but if the Vacancy Cost is €2,400, that's real money you didn't earn. Focus your attention wherever Vacancy Cost is highest.
  • Cross-reference Occupancy Rate with Avg Days to Fill. A property can have low occupancy for two very different reasons: (a) long individual vacancy gaps (slow to re-let), or (b) many short vacancy gaps (high tenant turnover). These need different solutions — (a) is a marketing / pricing problem, (b) is often a property-quality or management problem.
  • A high Vacancy Cost with low Avg Days to Fill is unusual. It would mean the property re-lets quickly but still has lots of vacancy — which usually indicates high turnover. Investigate why tenants aren't staying.
  • Zero values ("0 days", €0 cost) are the goal. They mean either back-to-back tenancy or a renewed lease — no gap at all. Celebrate the properties that consistently show zeros.
Managing the opt-in flag
  • Flag a property for occupancy tracking only if "% of the year occupied" is a meaningful measure for it. Long-term residential rentals: yes. Short-term holiday lets, owner-occupied homes, vacant land: no.
  • If a property's use changes, update the flag. A property that was a summer let last year and is now a long-term rental should be flagged on to start appearing on this page. Conversely, a rental that becomes a holiday let should be flagged off.
  • Where to toggle it: on the Properties module, edit the property and look for the "Include in Occupancy" checkbox.
Diagnosing Needs-Improvement properties
  • Vacancy Cost high and Avg Days to Fill high → slow-to-let problem. Likely pricing, marketing, or agent issues. Consider: is the rent above comparable market rates? Are the listing photos good? Is your letting agent proactive?
  • Occupancy Rate low but Avg Days to Fill low → high-churn problem. Tenants are leaving quickly. Why? Property condition? Rent inflation? Neighbourhood issues? Lease terms that don't attract stable tenants?
  • Occupancy Rate low and Vacancy Cost modest → low-rent property with chronic gaps. The revenue impact is limited by the low rent, but the property still isn't working. Consider whether it makes sense to keep it in the portfolio.
Acting on what you see
  • The View Historical Performance Trends button at the bottom of the page jumps to multi-year trend charts. Use it when a property looks problematic on this page and you want to see whether it's a recent regression or a long-term pattern.
  • Cross-reference with the Financial Indicators page. A property can have perfect occupancy but still have a poor Net ROI if expenses are eating into income. Use both dashboards together for the full picture.
  • Export Excel for stakeholder reviews. The Excel export from the Performance Modal is useful for periodic property portfolio reviews with accountants, investment partners, or co-owners.
This dashboard is about lost opportunity. Every day a property is vacant is a day you're not earning. The goal isn't just to spot underperformers — it's to translate the numbers into specific actions: adjust pricing, switch letting agent, address property condition, renew earlier, or consider repositioning the asset.
