What is this report?

The Financial Indicators dashboard is the benchmarking counterpart to the Profit & Loss Statement. Where the P&L tells you "how is the portfolio doing?", this report tells you "how is each property doing compared to the rest of the portfolio?"

It uses the same five KPIs as the P&L's Consolidated Financial Indicators block, but here they're calculated for every property individually and displayed side-by-side so you can see outliers, identify strong performers, and spot properties that need attention.

Four layers of visualisation

The dashboard presents the same data in four increasingly-detailed views:

  1. Consolidated Indicators cards — the portfolio-level totals, clickable for drill-down
  2. Property Selection panel — checkboxes to filter which properties are included
  3. Five mini bar charts — one per KPI, visually sorted best-to-worst with colour-coded performance
  4. Detailed Property Data table — every property with every KPI value, with sortable columns and a pinned Portfolio Totals row
When to use this report vs the P&L
  • Use the P&L when you want to see numbers — how much came in, how much went out, the month-by-month pattern
  • Use Financial Indicators when you want to see performance — how each property compares, which ones are outperforming or underperforming, where to focus attention

The two are complementary. The P&L gives you the quantitative picture; Financial Indicators gives you the comparative picture.

The Five Indicators

Each indicator measures a different dimension of property performance. All calculations are based on the selected year's data divided by purchase price (or another stable baseline).

1. Gross ROI (%)

Formula: Property Revenue ÷ Property Purchase Price × 100

What it tells you: How much revenue this property generates relative to what you paid for it, before expenses. A property generating €10,000/year in rent on a €200,000 purchase price has a Gross ROI of 5%. Useful for top-line efficiency and cross-property comparison.

Higher is better.

2. Net ROI (%)

Formula: (Revenue − Expenses) ÷ Property Purchase Price × 100

What it tells you: The real return — what's left as profit after all expenses, as a percentage of what you paid. This is the single most important number on the page. A property with high Gross ROI but much lower Net ROI has an expenses problem.

Higher is better.

3. Expenses to Revenue (%)

Formula: Expenses ÷ Revenue × 100

What it tells you: What percentage of each property's revenue gets consumed by expenses. A value of 30% means 30 cents of every euro earned go to costs. Lower is better. A property with Expenses-to-Revenue much higher than the portfolio average is a candidate for cost review — either its expenses are high or its revenue is low, and the drill-down helps you identify which.

4. Rent (€/m2)

Formula: (Property Revenue ÷ 12) ÷ Property Floor Area (m²)

What it tells you: Average monthly rent per square metre — the key normaliser. A 70m² property getting €700/month is doing the same €/m² as a 200m² property getting €2,000/month. Useful for spotting underpriced properties (compared to market rates in the same area) and for understanding whether a high-absolute-revenue property is actually performing well for its size.

Higher is better.

5. Value Increase (%)

Formula: (Current Value − Purchase Price) ÷ Purchase Price × 100

What it tells you: Capital appreciation — how much the property has grown in value since purchase. Drawn from the Property Valuations page. A property with low income ROI but strong capital appreciation might still be a great investment; one with both low income and low appreciation is the one to worry about.

Higher is better.

How the performance tiers work

For each indicator, every property is classified into one of three tiers relative to the portfolio average:

Excellent

Higher-is-better metrics (Gross ROI, Net ROI, Rent/m², Value Increase): value is 120% or more of the portfolio average.
Lower-is-better metrics (Expenses to Revenue): value is 80% or less of the portfolio average.

Good

Higher-is-better: value is between 80% and 120% of portfolio average — roughly in line with the rest of the portfolio.
Lower-is-better: value is between 80% and 100% of portfolio average.

Needs Improvement

Higher-is-better: value is less than 80% of portfolio average.
Lower-is-better: value is more than 100% of portfolio average.

Tiers are relative, not absolute. A property marked "Needs Improvement" isn't necessarily unprofitable — it just means it's trailing your own portfolio's average. If your whole portfolio has a 7% Gross ROI, a property with 5% is "Needs Improvement" here even though 5% is perfectly respectable in absolute terms. The tiers are there to help you rank within your portfolio, not to judge absolute performance.
Reading the Dashboard

The dashboard flows top-to-bottom in a natural reading order: portfolio summary → property filter → visual charts → detailed table.

Consolidated Indicators cards

The top strip shows the five portfolio-level KPIs as clickable cards. Each card shows the KPI's icon, label, and overall portfolio value (e.g. Gross ROI: 5.37%). The figures are the same numbers shown in the P&L's Consolidated Financial Indicators block.

Hover a card — it lifts slightly and shows "Click for details" as a tooltip. Click a card — it opens the Property Performance Details modal, explained fully in the next tab.

Property Selection panel

Identical to the Profit & Loss property selector. Every property in the portfolio as a checkbox, with Select All / Select None shortcuts. Ticking or un-ticking reloads the entire dashboard live.

The counter shows e.g. "10 of 10 active properties selected." All downstream calculations — the bar charts, the detailed table, the Portfolio Totals row, and the indicator drill-downs — recompute on the reduced scope.

The five mini bar charts

One chart per indicator, displayed in a responsive grid. Each chart has:

  • Chart title with icon and KPI label
  • Portfolio Average shown in the header in the indicator's accent colour
  • Bars sorted best-to-worst from left to right — no need to scan for extremes, they're always visually anchored at the edges
  • Colour-coded bars:
    • Green — Excellent tier
    • Yellow — Good tier
    • Red — Needs Improvement tier
  • Orange dashed line horizontally across the chart — the Portfolio Average benchmark
  • Rotated property labels on the X-axis (so they fit at any chart width)
Detailed Property Data table

The table at the bottom shows every selected property as a row, with a column for each KPI. Features:

  • Sortable columns — click any column header to sort by that KPI. Click again to reverse the order. The active column is highlighted teal and the header shows a directional arrow icon.
  • Striped rows — alternating shading for readability
  • Portfolio Totals row — pinned at the bottom in teal, showing portfolio-level values for comparison
  • Hover highlights — the row under your cursor is highlighted
The table's sort and the charts' sort are independent. The mini charts always auto-sort best-to-worst. The table starts unsorted and only sorts when you click a column. This means you can have the table sorted by one KPI while the charts show their own natural ordering.
The Property Performance Modal

Clicking any of the five Consolidated Indicator cards at the top of the dashboard opens the Property Performance Details modal — the detailed drill-down for that one indicator.

What the modal shows
  • Header block — KPI name, formula explanation, and Portfolio Average for this indicator
  • Performance legend — the three colour-coded tiers explained inline
  • Ranked property table with five columns:
    • # — rank position
    • Property Name
    • Performance — the KPI value for this property
    • vs Portfolio Avg — percentage difference from the portfolio average (e.g. +55.7% or −23.5%)
    • Performance Level — a coloured badge: EXCELLENT / GOOD / NEEDS IMPROVEMENT
  • Summary cards at the bottom — count of properties in each performance tier
How the ranking works

The table is automatically sorted best-to-worst — but "best" depends on the metric:

Higher-is-better metrics (Gross ROI, Net ROI, Rent/m², Value Increase): properties with the highest values are ranked first.
Lower-is-better metrics (Expenses to Revenue): properties with the lowest values are ranked first. This is because a lower expense ratio is better performance.

So the property at rank #1 is always your "best performer" for this KPI, and the property at the bottom is always the one that most needs attention. No need to mentally reverse the ordering for different metrics.

Row colour shading

Each row is subtly tinted based on its performance tier:

  • Light green background — Excellent
  • Light yellow background — Good
  • Light red background — Needs Improvement

This makes it easy to visually cluster properties by performance band and spot where the tier boundaries fall in your portfolio.

Export to Excel

The Export to Excel button in the modal footer downloads the ranked list as a properly-formatted .xlsx file. The export includes:

  • A header line with the indicator name
  • Generation date
  • Portfolio Average
  • The full ranked table: Rank / Property Name / Value / vs Portfolio Avg / Performance Level

The filename is automatic and descriptive, e.g. PropertyPerformance_grossROI_2026-04-19.xlsx. Useful for sharing with accountants, pulling into other reports, or archiving.

Practical Tips
Using the dashboard for portfolio analysis
  • Start with Net ROI as the primary signal. Of the five indicators, Net ROI is the most meaningful. The property at the top of that ranking is earning you the most relative to what you paid; the one at the bottom is earning the least. Everything else is context for why.
  • Cross-reference Net ROI with Expenses to Revenue. A property with low Net ROI and high Expenses to Revenue has a cost problem. A property with low Net ROI but normal Expenses to Revenue has a revenue problem. The combination tells you which lever to pull.
  • Rent (€/m²) reveals pricing issues. If a property has a healthy Gross ROI but a below-average Rent/m², it's technically performing well but probably underpriced for its size — review the rental agreement.
  • Value Increase is the "long game" indicator. A property with low short-term ROI but strong Value Increase is still a solid investment — the return is coming from appreciation, not income. Don't judge a property on income ROI alone.
  • Click the indicator cards for a focused view. Rather than scanning the bar charts and the detailed table together, click into the Performance Modal for one KPI at a time. The ranked list + tier breakdown gives a clearer picture than the dashboard overview.
Working with the performance tiers
  • Tiers are relative, not absolute. A property can be "Excellent" in a portfolio of under-performers, or "Needs Improvement" in a portfolio of exceptional performers. The tiers help you rank within your own portfolio, not benchmark against the market. Use with this context in mind.
  • Healthy portfolios have mostly Good, some Excellent, and few or no Needs-Improvement. If most properties are in the Needs Improvement tier for the same KPI, the portfolio itself might have a systematic issue, not individual properties.
  • Tier classifications can flip as you change the property filter. Removing strong performers from the selection shifts the portfolio average down — so the remaining properties may move up a tier. This is by design and can reveal interesting dynamics (e.g. "if I excluded my top 3 properties, everyone else would look a lot more average").
  • Use the Summary cards to assess portfolio shape. The count of Excellent / Good / Needs Improvement properties at the bottom of the modal gives you a quick health check for each KPI. Lots of Excellent = uneven performance (a few carrying the rest). Lots of Good = a balanced portfolio.
Actionable workflows
  • Monthly review: open the Net ROI modal. Check the top and bottom 2-3 properties. Ask: has anything changed from last month? Any property moved up or down the ranking? Investigate significant movements.
  • Annual planning: export all five indicators to Excel and pull them into your own planning workbook. Look for trends year-over-year.
  • Country comparison: use the Property Selection panel to filter down to just one country's properties at a time. Compare Net ROI and Expenses to Revenue across jurisdictions — differences often reveal tax structure or cost-base variations between markets.
  • Property-by-property investigation: select just one property, and you'll see that one property's values alongside the Portfolio Totals row. Useful for a focused deep-dive into a specific property's numbers.
Connecting back to the source
  • If a Net ROI looks wrong, go to the P&L. Filter the P&L to that one property and drill into the expense and revenue details. The P&L tells you why.
  • If a Value Increase looks wrong, go to Property Valuations. The indicator is driven by Purchase Price and Current Value — a stale Current Value distorts the number. Updating it also cascades through to the Pro-Rata distribution via the preview modal (see the Property Valuations help modal).
  • If a Rent/m² looks wrong, check the Property record. Floor area (m²) is stored on the Property, not here. A missing floor area means no Rent/m² can be calculated. Fix via the Properties module.
This dashboard answers the question "which property should I focus on?" The P&L answers "how did we do?"; the Financial Indicators answers "and which properties need my attention to do better next year?" Together, they give you both the number and the signal.
