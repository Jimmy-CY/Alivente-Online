What is Acquisitions?

Acquisitions keeps the property searches you run — the flat to renovate, the building plot — and every listing they find, scored against your own rules. Open it from Property Operations → Acquisitions.

Claude does the searching; the app keeps the results and does the scoring. The property sites block searches from a data centre like the one Alivente Online runs in, so the searches are Claude’s scheduled tasks. They write what they find here; the app never searches itself.

The tabs across the top of every page are the module’s pages: Overview, Scenarios, Price watch, Market snapshot and Search runs. A number on a tab is something new: new listings on Scenarios, price drops this week on Price watch.

The Overview shows the week at a glance — new listings, your shortlist, price drops and how the last search went — a card for each scenario, the latest activity, and which sites the last search could and could not search. Latest activity and Last search run start folded: click either to open it.

Run search now lists the active scenarios in a table — type and schedule beside each — all ticked: untick any you do not want this time (the header’s tick box ticks or clears them all) and press Start the search. Claude searches them straight away and the results arrive as each site reports. On a scenario’s own page the button ticks just that one. Until the search task’s start link is set up (see Search runs), the button says how to start a search in the Claude app instead.
Scenarios and their rules

A scenario is one search: what to look for, which websites to search (Where to look), how often, and how listings are scored. New scenario starts an empty one, in Build rules step by step, which asks what you are looking for and writes its rules. Nothing is saved until you press Save scenario.

Each rule is one of three types:

  • Must — a listing that clearly fails it is outside your rules: left out of the lists and the counts, never deleted.
  • Preferred — its points if met, none if not.
  • Scoring — points on a sliding scale: full inside a window and falling to nothing at the outer values, or in bands (full from 1995, half for 1985–94). A scenario’s points add up to 100.

Checked by says who answers it: the app compares facts itself; Claude judges only what the listing text alone can tell (open plan, condition, or a rule you write in plain words with Add a rule in plain words).

Where the property must be is the Location rule. Open it with the pencil and tick the areas; a listing anywhere else is outside your rules. An area that is not in the list can be added there: its name, the district, and any other spellings the sites use (Egkomi, Εγκωμη), which help match listings to it. The same areas are the scenario’s target areas everywhere else — the overview and the market comparison.

Buy or rent. An apartment or a house can be bought or rented; a plot is only bought. A rent scenario searches rental listings, its budget is the rent a month (no VAT), and a listing for sale cannot pass it — nor a rental a Buy scenario.

Build rules step by step asks you questions and writes the rules for you — see its own tab. A new, empty scenario opens in it.

Unknown is never “no”. A Must the listing does not answer caps the score (60 unless you change it). A listing with more unknown Musts than the limit (4 unless you change it) is not added. Musts in the same group — bathrooms, guest WC and en-suite — count once.

Live preview. As you change the rules, the editor shows how the listings already tracked would score, and what saving would do to them all: “Saving these rules would put 640 listings outside your rules (no longer shown) and bring back 3 that now pass”. When a change takes listings out or brings them back, Save scenario asks first. Saving re-scores every listing at once: no new search is needed, because the facts and the scores are kept apart. A listing outside your rules comes back by itself when it passes them again (a rule loosened, a fact confirmed). Rejected means rejected by you: it stays rejected until you clear your decision. A listing you shortlisted is never hidden by a rule change — it stays in sight with the rule it now fails named.

Build rules step by step

Under Criteria in the scenario editor, Build rules step by step asks what you want and writes the rules for you, with a short explanation at each step. A new, empty scenario opens straight in it. It starts from the rules already in the table, so you can run it again to adjust them. Nothing is saved until you press Save scenario, and every rule it writes can be changed afterwards with the pencil.

1. What are you looking for? An apartment, a house or a plot. For an apartment or a house, also Buy or Rent; a plot is only bought. Every question after this follows your answer. Choosing a different type, or Rent instead of Buy, starts that one fresh; the areas you ticked stay.

2. Where must it be? Tick the areas, or add one that is missing. This is the Location rule: a listing anywhere else is outside your rules.

3. Your budget. The most you would pay is a Must. To buy, that is the price including VAT (a “+VAT” listing has 19% added); to rent, the rent a month. The price you would love to pay earns points, falling to none at your most.

4. For an apartment or a house:

  • How big — the internal area, smallest and largest as Musts and an ideal range for points; covered balconies for an apartment, the plot size for a house.
  • Rooms — the fewest bedrooms and bathrooms you would accept, and the most bedrooms if you like.

4. For a plot:

  • How big a plot — smallest and largest as Musts, an ideal range for points. A plot has no rooms and no covered area.
  • What it costs per m² — the most per m² of land (a Must), the building density and site coverage you would like (they earn points), and € per buildable m² (the price divided by plot size × density), which earns points: full at or under one figure, none at or over another.

5. Which features matter? For each: Must have (a listing without it is outside your rules), Nice to have (it earns points) or Doesn’t matter. The list depends on what you are looking for:

  • Apartment to buy — the home (lift, parking spaces — you set how many — covered parking, storeroom, whole floor, guest WC, master en-suite, open plan, to renovate, built in or after a year you set, separate title deed), comfort and energy (energy class A or B, underfloor or central heating, air conditioning in all rooms, photovoltaics) and position (not on the ground floor, top floor or penthouse, roof garden, a view, south-facing, quiet street).
  • House to buy — private garden, swimming pool, parking, covered parking, storeroom, guest WC, en-suite, to renovate, year built, separate title deed, the same comfort and energy questions, and a view, south-facing, quiet street.
  • To rent — as to buy without title deed and to renovate, plus furnished, pets allowed, available now and a lease of 12 months or less.
  • Plot — separate title deed, two or more road frontages, legal paved road access, no road, path or river across it, flat and regular, not in a flood area, corner plot, water and electricity at the boundary. Building density and site coverage are asked with the costs, and earn points rather than reject.

Those marked checked by Claude are read from the listing text; the app compares the rest itself. The legal checks — separate title deed in the seller’s name, final approval certificate, mortgages or memos on the title, unlicensed additions, communal charges, Turkish-Cypriot land — are not rules: they are listed on every offer brief, for your lawyer.

6. Share 100 points between the rules that earn points — the ideal ranges and the nice-to-haves, so the list is different for a plot and a home. The total turns red until it is 100; Share evenly splits them for you.

7. Your rules. Every rule in plain words. Replace my rules clears the table and puts these in (a rule that matches one already there keeps it, so Claude’s answers to it are kept); Add to my rules keeps the table and adds these. It also sets the scenario’s property type and Buy or rent.

Skip this step leaves a step out: it writes no rules, and you add them by hand afterwards with Add criterion. Skipping the points keeps the suggested split. On a new scenario the last button is Use these rules; on one that already has rules it is Replace my rules, with Add to my rules beside it.

Lists of ticks are tables, one item a row: the areas in the wizard and in the Location rule, and the websites under Where to look. The tick box in the header row ticks or clears every row at once; it shows half-ticked when only some are.

Saving a scenario takes you to its results and asks: Run it now with the new rules, or Wait for the schedule (it shows when that is). The listings already tracked are re-scored straight away either way.

Score cap if a Must is unconfirmed: a listing that does not say whether it meets a Must — no word of a lift, say — is not rejected, but its score cannot go above this number out of 100 until the fact is known (100 = no cap). Most unknown Musts a listing may have: a listing that leaves more Musts unanswered than this is too unclear and is kept off the shortlist (0 = every Must must be confirmed).

A scenario’s results, and one property

Open a scenario to see its latest written summary, its key numbers, what’s holding listings back (the Musts most often failed or unconfirmed), and every listing within your rules. Filter, in the bar at the top, opens the filter panel, as elsewhere in the app: search by title or area, Area, Status (each with its count), price incl. VAT from and to, size (plot m² on a plot scenario) from and to, bedrooms on a home scenario, Opened (Not opened yet, or Opened, no decision yet) and Sort (score, price, date found, status, or €/m², low to high). Each filter that is on shows as a tag under the bar; its × clears it, and Clear All clears them all. A line under the bar says how many listings are outside your rules and not shown; Show them (or Status → Outside your rules) lists them. Search also looks through your own comments and reasons, so kitchen finds every listing you wrote that about. Under each title is your comment, shortened to a line; a listing you have not opened yet says Not opened.

Opened, and asked. The first time you open a listing’s page it counts as opened. If you then leave it by a link on the page — Back, a tab, the menu — without having shortlisted, rejected or commented on it, a pop-up asks what you thought: type a comment and Save comment, or Reject or Shortlist it with your words as the reason, or Not now. The browser’s own Back button and closing the tab cannot be asked; the filter’s Opened, no decision yet finds those listings later. Your comment is also in Your notes & viewings on the listing’s page.

The listings you rejected sit at the foot of the page, folded — click to open them; Status → Rejected lists them on their own. “Possibly the same … as” marks two listings in the same area within 2% on size and 5% on price — usually one home or plot listed by two agents; keep one and reject the other. Export CSV downloads the list. A plot scenario’s table shows each plot’s area, its price including VAT, its €/m² and its € per buildable m² — the price divided by the plot size times the building density.

VAT on plots. Most plot ads do not say whether VAT applies. Unless the ad says no VAT, a plot’s price is shown and judged including 19% VAT — marked VAT assumed — so nothing looks cheaper than it may be. A plot whose ad says “no VAT” is taken at its asking price.

A property has its scorecard rule by rule (✓ met, ✗ failed, ? unknown), its price history, the questions to ask the agent (built from the unconfirmed Musts — Copy questions puts them on the clipboard; Claude never contacts agents), its facts, how its price compares with the area median, its sources and agents, and your notes and viewings.

Shortlist and Reject… are your decisions, with a few words of why. A search or a re-score never changes a status you set; Clear my decision gives it back to the rules. Shortlisted listings are price-checked every day.

Build offer brief opens, in a pop-up on the same page, a one-page A4 PDF (with Download and Print): the asking price, the comparable listings and their median €/m², the price history and the questions to confirm. The suggested offer range and the points for and against appear once Claude has written them.

Price watch and the market snapshot

Price watch records every price seen, so you can see how long a property has been for sale and how often it has been cut. Watch rules choose what is re-checked daily (your shortlist, or your shortlist and the active listings) and when you are alerted: a fall of any size or of at least 3% or 5%, a watched listing that disappears, or a cut that makes a rejected listing pass.

Market snapshot is recorded every Monday from the listings seen that week: for each area and size band, the listings for sale, the new ones, the median asking price per m², the median price and the median days listed, with a line chart and a short commentary from Claude. These are asking prices, not sale prices.

The search runs

Search runs lists every search: where it ran (the cloud or your PC), how long it took, the sites searched, the listings checked and added, and how it ended: Running until the search reports its finish, then Complete (every site searched) or Incomplete (some were blocked or skipped); a run that never reported its finish shows Did not finish after three hours. Choose one to see each site’s result, the summary it sent, and any PC search that finished it. While a run is going, its sites and listings appear as the search reports them, not only at the end.

How the searches report to the app. Claude’s search tasks read your active scenarios from the app — their areas, websites and rules, and the listings each already tracks — and send back what they find: each listing’s facts as the listing states them, Claude’s answers to the rules it checks, and how each website went. The app does the scoring: a listing that clearly fails a Must, or leaves more Musts unanswered than the scenario allows, is not added; a price change is recorded and noted; a listing whose every link has gone is marked Removed. A status you set yourself is never changed. Each search appears here as a run, with its summary when it finishes.

Connecting them, once. The cloud search uses a private key that only Railway and Claude’s search environment know. Make a long random one (at least 32 characters) and put it on Railway as ACQ_INTAKE_KEY. In Claude, the search task’s environment (Alivente Search) holds the same key as a network secret for alivente.online, header X-Acq-Key, no prefix: Claude adds it to each request without ever seeing it. Never paste it in a chat, an email or a file. Until it is set, the app accepts nothing from the cloud search. If you ever change it, change it in both places.

The PC search reports as you. It runs in the Claude app’s own browser on your computer, which cannot hold the key, so it sends its results from a page of Alivente Online while you are signed in there — with the same protection as any form in the app. Sign in to Alivente Online once in the Claude app’s browser; nobody else’s session can report, and only someone who can edit Acquisitions.

Starting a search from the app. Run search now starts Claude’s search task through its start link. To set it up once: at claude.ai/code/routines open the search task, then Edit. Add to its prompt that a run started with a routine-fire-payload block from Alivente Online searches only the scenarios it names. In Trigger press +, then API. Copy the Fire URL, press Generate token (it is shown once), and put the two on Railway as ROUTINE_FIRE_URL and ROUTINE_FIRE_TOKEN. Never paste them anywhere else. If the token is ever regenerated, update the Railway setting. Each start counts towards your Claude usage.

Sites that block the cloud — marked PC: Bazaraki, Spitogatos, DOM, and any site whose search needs a real browser — are covered by the PC search. Start it with Start the PC search on the Overview or a run: the pop-up opens the PC search in Claude — press Run now there — with the Claude desktop app open, the computer awake and Alivente Online signed in in its browser. The pop-up then follows it: Waiting for the PC search to start, then each site as it reports, then what it added. If nothing has started after five minutes, it says what to check. Run search now follows the cloud search the same way. Which sites are PC is set in the site admin (alivente.online/admin/ → Acquisitions → Websites): tick Needs PC beside each and press Save once. A listing is only marked Removed when its page actually loaded and showed it gone, never because a site was blocked.
Who can do what

Can access Acquisitions opens every page: the scenarios, the listings, the market, the runs, and the offer brief. Can edit Acquisitions also creates and edits scenarios, shortlists and rejects, writes notes, logs viewings and sets the watch rules. Both are set per user in User Administration → Permissions.

Changes to scenarios, rules, your decisions on a listing and viewings are recorded in the activity log. The automatic re-scoring is not.
