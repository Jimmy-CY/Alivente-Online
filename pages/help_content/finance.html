Welcome to Financials

The Financials section is the heart of Alivente Online's analytical capability. Every euro in or out of your property portfolio — budgeted, actual, paid, overdue, appreciated, depreciated — eventually shows up somewhere on one of these twelve submodules.

The landing page splits cleanly into two halves:

Reports — 6 modules

Visual summaries, drill-downs, and analytics. This is where you view how the portfolio is performing: the P&L, cash flow forecast, ROI indicators, valuations, vacancy stats, and historical trends. Most of what you'll do on a routine basis happens here.

Configuration — 6 modules

The underlying data that feeds the reports: the types of revenue and expenses you track, how they're categorised, and the individual records themselves. Set this up once when you first use the system; update it periodically as things change.

How the two halves connect

Reports are built from Configuration. Every number on the P&L traces back to a Revenue or Expense record you've captured in Configuration. The relationship is:

  • Configuration → Records — you input Revenue and Expense records against each property
  • Records → Reports — the reports aggregate, analyse, and visualise those records
  • Reports → Decisions — the reports help you decide what to change in Configuration
Every submodule has its own Help button

This page is a navigator. The Reports and Configuration tabs give you a quick overview of each submodule, but for the detailed help — how to read the P&L, how Pro-Rata works, how the Performance Modal ranks properties — click into each submodule and open its own Help button.

Permissions: Most Financials modules are restricted to Superusers.
The Six Reports

Reports are your analytical views — where the data tells its story. Each has its own Help button with a full deep-dive.

Profit & Loss Statement

The central financial report. 12-column grid (Jan-Dec + Total + Monthly average) showing Revenue, Expenses, and Net Profit across the portfolio. Switch between Budget mode and any historical year. Drill into any figure to see per-property breakdowns. In Year mode, includes an Actual Expenses row showing approved and paid expenses. Five Financial Indicator KPIs at the bottom.

Forecasted Cash Outflows

12-month forward-looking view of expected expenses (budgeted). Bar chart with a click-through to per-month breakdowns. Useful for spotting "big bill months" (e.g. the annual company tax in June) and planning cash reserves.

Financial Indicators

The per-property benchmarking dashboard. Same five KPIs as the P&L (Gross ROI, Net ROI, Expenses to Revenue, Rent/m², Value Increase), but calculated for every property individually and compared against the portfolio average. Mini bar charts, sortable table, drill-down modal with performance tiers and Excel export.

Property Valuations

Records the Purchase Price and Current Value for each property — the foundation for ROI and Value Increase calculations. Editing a Current Value triggers a cascade preview showing how Pro-Rata expense distributions will shift across the affected Expense records.

Vacancy Management

Occupancy-focused sibling of Financial Indicators. Three KPIs — Occupancy Rate, Avg Days to Fill, Vacancy Cost — for every property that's flagged for occupancy tracking. Time Period selector (Past Year / Last 3 Years / All Time). Click a KPI card to drill into a ranked property list.

Performance Trends

Multi-year drill-down from Vacancy Management. Three line charts showing how Occupancy Rate, Avg Days to Fill, and Vacancy Cost have evolved year by year. Click any year to see a full breakdown including the individual vacancy periods with previous and next tenants.

The Six Configuration Modules

These are the building blocks that feed every report. Three revenue-related, three expense-related — each pair follows the same pattern: Types → Line Types → Records.

Revenue side
Revenue Types

Templates for when revenue occurs during the year. Each Type defines a 12-month Yes/No pattern (e.g. "Monthly", "Summer Months", "Q1 Only"). Reused across multiple revenue records so you don't re-specify the pattern every time.

Revenue Line Types

The category labels for revenue (Rental, Levies, etc.) — these are what appear as row labels on the P&L.

Revenues

The actual revenue records. Each one combines a property, a Line Type, a Type, and an amount — which together tell the system "this property generates €X in Rental, paid Monthly". Feeds the Revenue section of the P&L.

Expense side
Expense Types

Same as Revenue Types but for expenses. Yes/No month patterns for when expenses hit (e.g. "Monthly" for communal fees, "June Only" for annual company tax).

Expense Line Types

The category labels for expenses. Two flavours: standard (normal categories like Cleaning Costs, Electricity) and Pro-Rata (shared costs like bookkeeping, where the total is distributed across properties based on their Current Values). Changing a Pro-Rata amount triggers an automatic cascade preview.

Expenses

The budgeted expense records. Each combines a property, a Line Type, a Type, and an amount. For Pro-Rata Line Types, the amount is auto-distributed; for standard Line Types, you enter it per property. Feeds the Expenses section of the P&L.

Note: "Expenses" here is budgeted, not actuals. Actual one-off expenses (repairs, unplanned bills, etc.) are captured via the separate Expenses module on the sidebar — not this Configuration → Expenses page. Only approved and paid actuals flow into the P&L's "Actual Expenses" row.
Getting Started — the typical workflow

If you're setting up Financials for the first time, or onboarding someone new, here's the order of operations:

Phase 1 — Set up the categories (first-time only)
  1. Revenue Types — create your month patterns (Monthly, Summer, Q1, etc.)
  2. Revenue Line Types — create your revenue categories (Rental, Levies, etc.)
  3. Expense Types — create month patterns for expenses
  4. Expense Line Types — create your expense categories, and decide which are Pro-Rata
Tip: Types and Line Types are system-wide; you set them up once and reuse them across every property.
Phase 2 — Set up Property Valuations

Go to Property Valuations and record the Purchase Price and Current Value for every property. This is the foundation for ROI and Value Increase calculations, and it also determines how Pro-Rata expenses are distributed.

Phase 3 — Capture your data
  1. Revenues — for every property, record the expected Rental (and any other income) by combining a Line Type + Type + Amount
  2. Expenses — same process: record each budgeted expense per property. For Pro-Rata items, just set the total amount on the Line Type and the system distributes automatically.
Phase 4 — Review the Reports

With Configuration complete, the reports become meaningful:

  • Profit & Loss Statement — the overall picture: does the portfolio make money?
  • Forecasted Cash Outflows — when are the big-bill months coming up?
  • Financial Indicators — per-property benchmarking: which properties are outperforming/underperforming?
  • Vacancy Management — for rental properties, how well are they staying let?
  • Performance Trends — how has occupancy changed over the years?
Phase 5 — Ongoing maintenance

Most of the setup is one-time. Routine updates are minimal:

  • Monthly — approve and pay incoming expenses (via the sidebar Expenses module)
  • When rents change — update the Revenue record for that property
  • When expense contracts change — update the Expense record for that property
  • Annually — update Current Values in Property Valuations (triggers Pro-Rata recalculation automatically)
Every submodule has a dedicated Help button. If you get stuck at any step, open the page's own help modal for detailed, context-specific guidance.
Practical Tips
Navigating efficiently
  • Use this landing page as your financial command centre. When you come to the system to "check the numbers", this is where you start. Every substantive financial question has a home on this page.
  • Most routine work is in Reports. Configuration is for setup and change-management — if you're just reviewing performance, you'll rarely need to visit the Configuration cards.
  • When Reports show something wrong, Configuration is where you fix it. An incorrect P&L number? Check the relevant Revenue or Expense record. A wrong Pro-Rata distribution? Check the Expense Line Type or Property Valuation.
Which report for which question
  • "How did the portfolio do this year?" → Profit & Loss Statement (year mode)
  • "What does the budget say for next year?" → Profit & Loss Statement (budget mode)
  • "Which property is performing best/worst?" → Financial Indicators
  • "When is my next big-bill month?" → Forecasted Cash Outflows
  • "How quickly did we re-let Property X?" → Vacancy Management (current view) or Performance Trends (history)
  • "What's my portfolio's capital appreciation?" → Property Valuations (raw data) or Financial Indicators (as a KPI)
Keeping data fresh
  • Update Current Values at least annually. Stale values undercut the accuracy of both Value Increase and Pro-Rata distributions. Set a calendar reminder for the first week of each year.
  • When you add a new property, cascade through. New property → add to Properties module → record Purchase Price and Current Value in Property Valuations → add Revenue records → add Expense records. Then check the P&L to confirm it's showing up correctly.
  • Don't forget the Include in Occupancy flag. On the Properties module, each property has an opt-in flag for occupancy tracking. Only flagged properties appear on Vacancy Management and Performance Trends — so if a new rental property is missing from those pages, check the flag.
  • Use the cascade previews confidently. When you edit a Pro-Rata amount or a Current Value, the preview modal shows exactly what will change before you commit. Review the preview, then save.
Cross-module awareness
  • The sidebar Expenses module is different from the Configuration → Expenses card. The sidebar one is for actual expenses (repairs, one-off bills, purchases); the Configuration one is for budgeted recurring expenses. Both feed the P&L but through different rows.
  • Approval and payment workflows matter. Only approved and paid actual expenses appear on the P&L's Actual Expenses row. If a number looks low, check the Expenses module for items stuck in approval or payment queues.
  • Invoices are separate. The Invoices module on the sidebar handles rental invoices to tenants — not expense invoices. Don't confuse the two.
Financials is your single source of truth for portfolio performance. Keep the Configuration clean and current, and the Reports will give you a reliable picture of how your investments are doing. Every other Alivente feature ultimately supports the story told here.
