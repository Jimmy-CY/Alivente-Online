What is the Suppliers module?

The Suppliers module is your central directory of contractors, service providers and trade contacts who work on your properties. Whenever you need to call a plumber, an electrician, an air-conditioning technician or your local property manager — this is the place to find their details.

Typical suppliers you might track:

  • Handymen — general repairs and maintenance
  • Plumbers — water, leaks and bathroom work
  • Electricians — wiring, fittings, electrical issues
  • Air-conditioning technicians — servicing and repairs
  • Pool maintenance — cleaning and chemical balancing
  • Fumigators — pest control
  • Property managers — on-the-ground representatives
  • Builders / construction firms — larger structural work
Why keep this list?
Having vetted, ready-to-call suppliers per country and per trade saves time when something breaks. No hunting through old emails or text messages.
Add New Supplier

Click the Add New button (top-right) to add a supplier. You'll be asked for the following:

  • Contact Person — the individual you actually call (e.g. "Costa (Agia Thekla)")
  • Contact Number — phone number, ideally with country code
  • Email Address — optional, leave blank if none
  • Company Name — if they trade under one (otherwise leave as None)
  • Role / Service — what they do (Plumber, Electrician, Property Manager, etc.)
  • Country — Cyprus, Greece or Spain
Edit an Existing Supplier

In the supplier table, click Edit in the Details column for the supplier you want to update. The same form opens with the existing details pre-filled. Make your changes and click Save.

Delete a Supplier (superuser only)

The red trash icon in the Delete column appears only for superusers. Clicking it opens a confirmation popup showing the supplier's details (Contact Person, Company, Role, ID).

Permanent action: Deleting a supplier cannot be undone. All associated data is removed. If you're not sure, edit the entry instead.
Using the Filter Panel

The Supplier Filters panel sits above the table. Click the panel header to expand or collapse it. Two filters are available:

Search Contact Person

Type a name (or part of one) and press Enter or click the search button.

Example: typing Vasilis will match both "Vasilis" and "Vasilis Aggelatos".

Country

Filter by country (Cyprus, Greece or Spain). Selecting a country auto-submits the form. Choose All Countries to remove this filter.

Active Filter Tags

When filters are applied, they appear as removable tags below the filter form. Click the small × on any tag to remove that single filter.

Clear All

The Clear All button (top-right of the filter panel) resets every filter back to its default and reloads the full supplier list.

Combine filters: The two filters work together. For example, search Property Manager in country Cyprus to narrow the list to property managers in Cyprus only.
What each row button does
Details — Edit

Opens the supplier's record in edit mode. Use this to update phone numbers, change role, switch country or correct typos.

Report — View

Opens a clean, printable Supplier Details Report showing the supplier's full profile in three sections:

  • Contact Information — phone and email
  • Company Details — company name and role
  • Location — country
Delete (superuser only)

Opens the delete-confirmation popup. The supplier is only removed once you click Delete Permanently in the popup — clicking the trash icon alone does nothing destructive.

Best Practices
  • Use a recognisable contact name — if you know two people called "Costa," disambiguate them in the contact field (e.g. Costa (Agia Thekla)) so you can tell them apart at a glance.
  • Always include the contact number — an email-only supplier is hard to reach in a hurry. Include the country code where possible.
  • Be consistent with the Role / Service — pick a short label and stick with it (Plumber, not "Pluming Guy" or "Mr. Fix-It"). Consistent labels make filtering far more useful.
  • Set the Country accurately — suppliers don't usually travel between countries, so country becomes your most useful filter when an issue arises.
  • Edit, don't delete, when a supplier becomes inactive — consider keeping them with a note in the role (e.g. "Plumber — retired") instead of deleting, so historic records still make sense.
  • Use the Report view to share details — the printable report is handy when you need to forward supplier info to a tenant or property manager.
  • Cross-link with Issues — once a supplier is in this directory, they can be assigned to property issues so the right tradesperson is notified for the right job.
Can't see the Delete column? The Delete option only appears for users with superuser privileges. If you need to delete a supplier and don't see the column, ask an administrator.
What is the Invoices page?

This page shows every open (unpaid) invoice in the system — one row per outstanding invoice, with the tenant, the amount owed, and how many days overdue it is.

Once you mark an invoice as paid, it disappears from this list. So the page acts as a live worklist of money still to be collected.

How invoices get here
  • Automatically — the system generates a fresh invoice for every active tenant on the 1st of each month.
  • Manually — a Superuser can click Generate Invoices in Administration to create any missing invoices on the fly (for example, if the 1st-of-month run was missed, or a tenant was added mid-month).
Related screens: the Notifications Dashboard shows the count of overdue invoices at a glance, and Administration → Notification Settings controls who is emailed when an invoice is marked paid.
Superuser only: Regular users see an Access Restricted message on this page.
The eight columns

Each row is one open invoice. Columns left to right:

Property

The property this invoice is for.

Country

Country where the property is located (Cyprus, Greece, Spain, etc.).

Tenant

The tenant who owes the rent.

Amount

Monthly rent, displayed in € with no decimals (right-aligned, monospace for easy column scanning).

Invoice Date

When the invoice was generated. For automatic invoices this is normally the 1st of the month.

Due Date

When payment is due. Calculated from the invoice date plus the tenant's payment terms (which are set on the tenant's record).

Days Overdue

How many days past the due date this invoice is. Shown in red bold when greater than zero — a clear visual cue that the invoice needs chasing.

Mark as Paid

A "Paid" link — click it once payment has been received to settle the invoice. See the Mark as Paid tab for what happens next.

Filtering the list

The Invoice Filters panel sits above the table. Click the panel header to expand or collapse it. Two filters are available:

Property

Narrow the list to invoices for a single property. Auto-submits on change — you don't need to click anything else.

Tenant

Narrow the list to invoices for a single tenant. Useful when chasing one specific person/company across multiple properties (where applicable). Also auto-submits.

Active filter tags

When filters are applied, they appear as removable tags below the dropdowns. Click the × on any tag to remove that single filter and reload the list.

Clear All

The Clear All button (top-right of the filter panel) resets both filters and reloads the full list of open invoices.

Combine filters: Property and Tenant filters work together. Picking both shows only invoices that match both conditions.
Marking an invoice as paid

When you've confirmed that the tenant has paid, click the Paid link in the right-hand column. Three things happen:

1. The invoice is closed in the system

The invoice's status flips to Paid and it is recorded as settled in the financial records.

2. The row disappears from this list

This page only shows open invoices, so a paid invoice is removed from view immediately.

3. A notification email is sent

An Invoice Marked as Paid email is sent to whoever is configured in Administration → Notification Settings for that notification type. This typically goes to the person who tracks invoice payments and reconciles accounts.

Confirm payment first: Only click Paid after you've actually verified the money has landed in the bank. There's no easy "Unpaid" reversal from this page.
Best Practices
  • Make this a weekly check — open the page once a week. Anything in red is a phone call or email to make.
  • Red Days Overdue is the only thing that matters at a glance — sort your attention by that. Recent invoices (0 days overdue) are not a concern yet.
  • Pair this with the Notifications Dashboard — the Dashboard shows the overdue invoice count without you having to open this page. Use the Dashboard for awareness, this page for action.
  • Always verify payment before clicking Paid — check your bank statement, then mark. Marking a row as Paid removes it — reversing it is much harder than waiting until you're certain.
  • Use filters when chasing — if you're calling one tenant, filter by them so you can see all their open invoices in one go.
  • Empty list is a good thing — when this page is empty, every tenant is fully paid up. That's the goal.
  • Configure the "Invoice Marked as Paid" notification — in Administration → Notification Settings, set TO and CC recipients so the right people are kept in the loop when payments come in.
  • If the 1st-of-month auto-run didn't fire — head to Administration → Generate Invoices and click it. Safe to click any time; it only creates invoices that don't already exist.
Expecting an invoice that isn't appearing? Check (1) the tenant has an active lease covering this period, (2) the 1st-of-month auto-run fired (or click Generate Invoices in Administration), and (3) the tenant's monthly rent is set on their record.
What is Petty Cash?

Petty Cash is a simple running ledger of small cash transactions related to property management. It tracks every euro that goes in and out of your physical petty cash float — with a live closing balance at the bottom of the table.

Typical things you'd record here:

  • Income — opening balance, top-ups (cash added to the float by Alivente), reimbursements paid in cash
  • Expenses — small property-related purchases paid in cash: cleaning supplies, small repairs, tips, parking, minor materials, postage, etc.
Why use this? Larger expenses go through the Expenses module with proper approval workflows and invoices attached. Petty Cash is for the small day-to-day stuff that wouldn't justify the overhead — the kind of thing you'd otherwise scribble on a receipt and forget about.
The closing balance should match your physical cash: If you reconcile properly, the green/red number at the bottom of the table is exactly what you should be holding in the petty cash tin or envelope at any moment.
The four columns
Date

When the transaction happened (not necessarily when it was entered into the system).

Description

Free-text description of the transaction. Best practice is to prefix with the property name when applicable — for example "Apolloneon — Demetri: Carrying washing machine upstairs" or "Lykavitto: Princess cleaned storeroom". Non-property entries (Opening Balance, Alivente Top-Up) skip the prefix.

Amount

The value of the transaction. Color-coded:

  • Green — Income (cash coming in, positive number)
  • Red, prefixed with − — Expense (cash going out)
Income / Expense

A clear text label of which type the row is — redundant with the colour but improves scannability and works for users with colour-vision difficulty.

CLOSING BALANCE row

The bottom row of the table is always the cumulative running balance — every income added, every expense subtracted, from the very first row to the last.

  • Green closing balance = positive cash on hand (the normal state)
  • Red closing balance = negative balance (something's wrong — either a missed top-up or a data-entry mistake)
Adding a new transaction

Click Add New (top-right) to open the Add Transaction form. It's a single row of four fields:

Date

Date picker (dd/mm/yyyy). Pick the date the transaction actually happened, not today — especially important when entering older receipts late.

Transaction Description

Free text. Use the "Property: detail" convention so future-you can scan the ledger and instantly see which property each cost relates to.

Amount

Enter the amount as a positive number. Don't add a minus sign — the Income / Expense dropdown handles whether the amount is added or subtracted from the balance.

Income / Expense

Dropdown — pick Income for cash coming in (top-ups, opening balance) or Expense for cash going out. This determines the colour and sign in the table.

Click Save to commit the transaction. Back returns to the ledger without saving.

Common mistake: Don't enter -30.00 in the Amount field with Expense selected — the system will subtract the negative, effectively adding 30 to your balance. Always enter 30.00 as Amount and let the Expense type handle the minus.
Best Practices
  • Reconcile regularly — physically count the cash in the petty cash tin and compare to the closing balance on screen. They should match. If they don't, find the missing transaction.
  • Use the "Property: detail" description format — e.g. Lykavitto: Bought rubbish bags @ Green Tree. Makes audit and review trivial. Skip the prefix for non-property entries (Opening Balance, top-ups).
  • Be consistent with naming — pick one form for top-ups (e.g. "Alivente Top-Up") and stick with it. Inconsistent variants like "Alivente: Top Up", "Alivente Top-Up", "Alivente top up" make the ledger harder to read at a glance.
  • Always positive amounts — enter 30, not -30. The Income / Expense type does the work.
  • Enter as soon as possible — the longer between the cash event and the entry, the more likely you'll forget what it was for. Phone-photo of the receipt + same-day entry is the gold standard.
  • Keep the physical receipts — the digital ledger is your record but receipts are the backup. File them by month in case you ever need to evidence a transaction.
  • Top up before you run out — if the closing balance is getting low, request an Alivente Top-Up before you're stuck unable to pay for something small.
  • Larger spend → Expenses module — if a transaction is big enough to warrant approval, an invoice or a paper trail beyond a receipt, record it in the Expenses module instead of here.
Closing balance gone red? Either a top-up was missed or an expense was entered twice. Scan recent rows for duplicates first, then check whether a recent top-up made it in.
What is the Dashboard?

The Property Dashboard is a visual, property-centred hub. Pick one property from your portfolio and every relevant view of it — legal documents, financial performance, tenant, expenses, issues, valuation — is one click away.

Use it as the starting point when you want to investigate a single property end-to-end, rather than jumping in and out of module-by-module screens from the main menu.

What's on screen

The dashboard is organised into three sections, each grouping the cards that naturally belong together:

  • Property — physical and legal aspects (Property Report, Title Deed, Valuation, Issues)
  • Tenant — who lives there and lease status (Tenant Report, Lease, Invoices, Lease Renewals)
  • Financials — money flows and performance (Revenues, Actual Expenses, Budgeted Expenses, Profit & Loss)
Cards are dim until you pick a property. That's deliberate — there's nothing meaningful to show until the dashboard knows which property you want to look at.
Three steps to use the Dashboard
1. Click the property selector

A modal opens listing every property in your portfolio.

2. Pick a property from the list

The modal closes, the selector turns darker teal and shows your property name, and every card lights up — the dashboard is now armed for that property.

3. Click any module card

You're navigated to the relevant view for the selected property.

Switching property

Click the property selector again at any time to swap to a different property. All cards reflect the new selection immediately — no need to navigate away.

Deep linking

You can pre-select a property by adding ?property=ID to the dashboard URL. Useful when linking from other parts of the system.

Keyboard

Esc closes the property picker modal.

The Property section

The four physical and legal aspects of the property:

Property Report

A multi-section snapshot: Property Address, Specifications (m², year built), Utility Accounts, Insurance & Status, and an Asset Summary with statistics, financial summary, assets grouped by category, and a full Asset Details table with warranty expiry dates.

Title Deed

View-only. Displays the uploaded title deed PDF inline. To upload, replace or delete the deed itself, use the Properties module.

Valuation

Compares Purchase Price against Current Value, with the gain/loss in € and as a percentage, plus a green/red trend arrow.

Issues

Maintenance, repairs and any open issues for this property. When there are none, shows a green "Excellent! No issues have been reported" empty state.

The Tenant section

Everything about who's currently renting the property and the state of their lease:

Tenant Report

The current tenant: Type (Individual or Company), Contact Information, Lease Period (start/end dates), Financial Information (deposit, monthly rent, levies, payment terms), and Lease Details (rental type, renewal clause, notice period, renewal status, active flag).

Lease

View-only. Inline PDF display of the current lease agreement. Page title shows the property and tenant in parentheses for context.

Invoices

Open invoices for this property's current tenant. Header shows the Total Amount Due in red. Each row lists Invoice Date, Due Date, Days Overdue, Amount, and a red Overdue badge. Scoped to this property only — for the system-wide list, use the main Invoices module.

Lease Renewals

The renewal state of the current lease. While active, shows a green "Current lease is active. No renewal action required at this time." together with the tenant name and end date. As expiry approaches, the screen prompts for renewal action.

The Financials section

Money in, money out, and the resulting performance:

Revenues

Breakdown of the property's revenue lines — typically Rental and Levies — with Revenue Type (Monthly, Yearly) and Amount. Pulled from the tenant's lease data.

Actual Expenses

A table of expenses actually incurred, with Date, Description, Amount, and a green invoice icon that opens the scanned receipt PDF. A Year filter sits in the top-right; the bottom row sums to Total Actual Expenses for [year].

Budgeted Expenses

The planned expense schedule. Each row: an Expense Line Type (Communal Fees, Company Tax, Insurance, Property Manager, etc.), an Expense Type indicating when (a specific month like June, or Monthly), and the budgeted Amount. This is the baseline that Actual is compared against.

Profit & Loss

A full 12-month P&L statement: Revenue rows, Expenses rows, Net Profit — monthly breakdown plus Total and Monthly-average columns. A Budget toggle switches between Actual and Budget views. Below the table, a Financial Indicators panel surfaces Gross ROI, Net ROI, Expenses to Revenue, Rent €/m² and % Value Increase.

Why split Expenses into Actual and Budgeted? Comparing the two is the heart of expense control. Looking at either alone only tells half the story.
Best Practices
  • Always pick a property first — everything else is locked until you do. The dim cards are a deliberate hint.
  • Use this as your starting point per property — if you're investigating one specific property end-to-end, the Dashboard is faster than going module-by-module from the sidebar.
  • Compare Actual vs Budgeted Expenses — click both in turn to spot variances. Big swings either way are worth investigating.
  • Switch properties without leaving the page — click the selector at the top. Faster than going Back and re-entering.
  • Use deep links — bookmark ?property=ID URLs for properties you check often.
  • Profit & Loss is the executive view — the full 12-month grid plus the Financial Indicators row gives a property's complete financial picture in one screen. If you only have time for one card per property, it's this one.
  • Use the Budget toggle on Profit & Loss — flip between Actual and Budget to see over- and under-performance against the plan.
Cards still dim after picking a property? Try re-opening the picker and selecting again. If the issue persists, refresh the page.
What is the Tenants page?

The Tenants page is the central register of every tenant who has ever occupied a property in the portfolio — current and past. Each row links a tenant to a property and shows whether that tenancy is currently active.

From this single screen you can:

  • See every tenant-property pairing at a glance, filtered however you like
  • Add a new tenant (superuser only)
  • Edit any tenant's details — including the powerful Duplicate for Renewal shortcut
  • Delete a tenant (superuser only, with a strong confirmation prompt)
  • View a full Tenant Details Report as a print-friendly summary
  • View the scanned lease agreement PDF inline in a popup
  • Jump to three related reports: Lease Timeline, Open Invoices, Lease Renewals
Superuser-only actions: Add, Edit and Delete are all restricted to Superusers. Regular Users can still view tenants, reports and agreements.
The six columns
Tenant

The tenant's name. For Individual tenants this may be a joint name (e.g. "Chrystalla Katelari and Antigoni Andreou"); for Company tenants it's the registered company name.

Property

The property the tenant is linked to. One tenant record ties to one property — if a tenant has occupied multiple properties over time, they appear as multiple rows.

Active

Active = this is the current tenant in the property. Inactive = a previous tenant whose lease has ended.

Details

For Superusers: two icon buttons — blue pencil to Edit, and red bin to Delete. Regular users see a disabled greyed-out icon.

Report

A View button that opens the Tenant Details Report — a clean, print-friendly summary of the tenant, contact info, lease period, financial terms and lease details.

Agreement

A View button that opens the scanned lease agreement PDF in a popup viewer (with a Download option). If no agreement has been uploaded, shows "Unavailable" in grey.

Three filters, working together

Click the Tenant Filters panel header to expand the filter row. Three dropdowns, all auto-submit on change:

Property

Narrow the list to tenants of a single property. Only properties marked Available for Rent appear in the dropdown.

Tenant

Jump straight to one named tenant — useful when you need to check their details or lease.

Status

Show only Active tenants (current occupants) or only Inactive (historical tenants). Default shows all.

Active filter tags & Clear All

Applied filters show as removable tags under the filters. Click the × on any tag to remove just that filter. The Clear All button (top-right of the panel) resets everything.

Common workflow: Filter by Status = Active to see only currently occupied properties. Flip to Inactive to see tenancy history.
Adding a new tenant

Click Add New (top-right) to open the Add Tenant form. The edit form covers the same fields, grouped into logical sections:

Tenant Type & Name

Type — Individual or Company. Tenant — the tenant's display name (for individuals, can be a joint name).

Contact Details

Contact Person, Contact Number (can list multiple, comma or " and " separated) and Email Address (same — multiple addresses accepted for joint tenants).

Lease Period

Lease Start Date and Lease End Date — date pickers defining the contracted tenancy window.

Financials

Rental Type (Monthly / Yearly), Deposit, Rental (rent amount), Levies (communal charges) and Rental Payment Terms (days — enter 0 for Immediate).

Renewal Details

Renewal Option (Yes/No), Renewal Period (notice in days) and Renewal Status (Pending, Accepted, Declined).

Status & Property Link

Tenant Status (Active/Inactive) and Property — pick which property this tenant occupies.

Duplicate for Renewal (yellow button, edit form)

When editing an existing tenant, a Duplicate for Renewal button creates a new tenant record pre-populated from the existing one — perfect for creating the next lease period when a tenant renews. Saves retyping contact details, property link and financial terms.

Delete is destructive: A strong confirmation popup warns you that deleting will remove the tenant record and related vacancy periods. Prefer marking inactive over deleting — you'll usually want the history.
Four reports and views

The top-right buttons and per-row buttons open five different views. All are read-only.

Tenant Details Report (per-row "View")

A clean print-friendly summary of one tenant: Type, Contact Information, Lease Period, Financial Information (Deposit, Monthly Rent, Levies, Payment Terms), and Lease Details (Rental Type, Renewal Clause, Notice Period, Renewal Status, Active flag). Dated with today's date in the header.

Agreement viewer (per-row "View")

Opens the scanned lease agreement PDF inline in a popup modal, with the tenant's name in the title bar. Includes a Download button and a fallback link to open the PDF in a new tab if the inline viewer fails. Non-PDF files (JPG, PNG) display as images; other types show a "cannot preview — please download" placeholder.

Lease Timeline (top-right button)

A visual Gantt-style calendar showing every lease across every property as horizontal coloured bars over a timeline.

  • ■ Green — Active Lease
  • ■ Grey — Past Lease
  • ■ Red — Expired (Still Marked Active) — a data-quality alert

A red "today" line runs vertically through the calendar. Controls: Previous/Next Month, Zoom In/Out, Fit All, and a Show Inactive Leases toggle.

Open Invoices (Debtors Age Analysis) (top-right button)

An ageing-bucket report of everyone who still owes you money, broken into Current (0–30 days), Past Due 31–60 days, 61–90 days, and 91+ days. Per-tenant totals in the leftmost column, grand totals across the bottom. Link on each amount drills into the detail.

Lease Renewals Report (top-right button)

Portfolio-wide list of upcoming lease expirations requiring renewal action, and any currently vacant properties. When nothing needs attention, shows a clean empty state: "No lease renewals or vacant properties to report at this time."

Best Practices
  • Use Duplicate for Renewal when a lease renews — don't retype contact details or rental amounts. Duplicate from the existing tenant, update the new dates and any price changes, and save.
  • Don't delete past tenants — set them to Inactive instead. You'll want the history for audit, financial records, and Lease Timeline visibility. Deletion removes vacancy-gap calculations too.
  • Red bars on the Lease Timeline are data problems — they mean a tenant is still marked Active even though their lease has expired. Chase these down: either renew the lease or flip the tenant to Inactive.
  • Pair Status = Active with the Lease Timeline — filter to Active tenants here for the textual list, then open the Lease Timeline for the visual spatial view. They reinforce each other.
  • Chase Open Invoices by age bucket — the 91+ days column is your urgency queue. Start there each week.
  • Always upload the scanned lease agreement — the Agreement column says Unavailable otherwise. Without it, staff can't verify terms without hunting for the paper copy.
  • Confirm the Property link before saving — assigning a tenant to the wrong property will distort every downstream report (invoices, P&L, Lease Timeline).
  • Use joint names for multi-occupant leases — the system accepts "Name A and Name B" throughout (tenant name, contact, email). Keeps notifications going to both leaseholders.
  • Renewal Status = Pending is a to-do flag — means you haven't yet heard back or decided. Flip to Accepted or Declined once you know, so the Lease Renewals report stays clean.
Deleting a tenant is irreversible: vacancy periods get removed, gap calculations recalculate, and dashboard metrics update. If in doubt, mark Inactive. The Disable-equivalent for tenants.
What is the Issues page?

Issues is the central log of everything that needs attention across the portfolio — maintenance requests, tenant complaints, repairs, meetings, and anything else worth tracking. Every issue has a running Comments thread for updates, a Status that moves through a lifecycle, and a Date Logged for reporting.

What you can do here
  • Browse every open and historical issue across every property
  • Filter and search by text, country, property or status
  • Sort by Property, Date Logged or Status (click the column headers)
  • Add a new issue
  • Open Comments on any issue to read the discussion thread, post updates, and change status
  • Delete an issue permanently (Superuser only)
  • Generate three reports: Comments Report, Resolved Issues Report, Friday Status Report
Status lifecycle

Three statuses with a clear lifecycle:

  • Unresolved — the default state when an issue is first logged. Stays here while the team is actively working on it.
  • Resolved — the issue has been fixed. Kept in the list for history and reporting.
  • Issue — a stuck / parked state. Used when the team hasn't been able to resolve something in an acceptable timeframe and no clear solution exists. Flags the item for escalation or senior review — it's not just "open for longer", it's "we need a different approach".
The columns

One row per issue. Columns shown left-to-right:

Property (sortable)

The property the issue relates to. Click the column header to sort alphabetically A→Z or Z→A.

Issue

The short heading — e.g. "Water Leak", "Repair of elevator", "Parking Bay". Keep these concise; full detail belongs in Description and Comments.

Description

The longer explanation. Think of it as the "statement of the problem" when the issue was first raised — it doesn't update as the story evolves; that's what Comments are for.

Date Logged (sortable)

When the issue was first raised (dd/mm/yyyy). Click to sort oldest-first or newest-first.

Status (sortable)

Coloured badge (Issue / Unresolved / Resolved). Sort is urgency-ordered — Unresolved first, then Issue, then Resolved — so the most pressing items rise to the top.

Comments

A Comments button that opens the comment thread for this issue (see the Comments tab).

Delete (Superuser only)

A red bin icon. Only visible to Superusers. Opens a confirmation popup with full issue details before deleting.

Default sort: Property (A→Z), with Status as the tiebreaker (Unresolved first, Resolved next, Issue/stuck items last). Click any sortable header to change.
Four filters

Click the Issue Filters panel header to expand. All auto-submit on change.

Search

Full-text search across the Issue heading and Description fields. Type your query and either press Enter or click the teal search icon button.

Country

Cyprus, Greece or Spain — matches on the property's country.

Property

Narrow to a single property.

Status

Resolved, Unresolved or Issue. Default shows all.

Active tags & Clear All

Applied filters appear as removable tags below the dropdowns. Click × on any tag to drop that single filter. Clear All (top-right of the panel) resets everything.

Column sorting

Three columns are sortable: Property, Date Logged, Status. Click the header once for ascending, again for descending. Arrow icons show the current sort direction. Status sort is lifecycle-ordered (Unresolved → Resolved → Issue), not alphabetical — active work first, completed work next, stuck items last.

Common workflow: Set Status = Unresolved, then sort by Date Logged (oldest first). That's your "stale issues to chase" list — what should already have been resolved by now.
The Comments page

Clicking the Comments button on any row opens a dedicated page for that issue, showing:

  • The issue heading, description in brackets, date logged, property and status at the top
  • A text box to enter a new comment, a Save Comment button, and a Change Status dropdown (Resolved / Unresolved / Issue) to update status in the same step
  • A reverse-chronological thread of every comment, colour-coded by author type
Comment colour coding

Comments in the thread alternate colour based on who wrote them — a subtle background tint makes the back-and-forth easy to follow. Each comment is automatically prefixed with the date and the author's initials (e.g. (DM), (SS)) based on who's logged in — you never type these yourself. Just write the comment content; the system handles attribution.

Why it's a thread, not an "update" field

Every comment is preserved permanently. The issue description doesn't change; the thread grows. This gives you a full audit trail of what was tried, what happened, what was decided — invaluable when reviewing old issues or handing work to a new staff member.

Change Status + Comment in one go: when you mark an issue Resolved, always add a closing comment explaining what happened. Don't just flip the status silently — the audit trail suffers.
Adding a new issue

Click Add New (top-right) to open the Add Issue form. Five fields:

Property Name

Dropdown of all properties. Pick the one this issue relates to.

Date Logged (auto-filled)

Defaults to today's date. Read-only in most cases.

Issue Status (auto-filled)

New issues default to Unresolved. Can be changed later via the Comments page.

Issue Heading

Short, specific title — "Water Leak", "Fridge does not work", "Repair of elevator". This is what appears in every list and report.

Issue Description

A longer description — the full context at the time of logging. Think of it as "everything I know about this problem right now". Subsequent developments go in Comments.

Save commits the new issue; Back cancels without saving.

Deleting an issue (Superuser only)

Click the red bin icon in the Delete column. A confirmation popup opens showing:

  • Property, Status, Date Logged, Issue ID
  • Issue Heading and Description
  • A prominent warning that the action is permanent

Click Delete Permanently to confirm, or Cancel to back out.

Delete removes all comments too. The entire thread goes with the issue. Prefer leaving old Resolved issues in place — they're historically useful and don't clutter anything if you filter to Unresolved.
Three reports, each with its own purpose

Three teal buttons at the top-right open three different reports. Each first shows an options modal to narrow the output.

Comments Report

Shows every comment (not issue) posted in a chosen time window. Time period options:

  • Last 7 Days
  • Last 30 Days
  • Last 90 Days
  • All Comments

Output: one row per comment with Comment text, the parent issue link and description, Date, Property, Status, User (author initials) and a Delete bin. Author rows are colour-coded — Admin Comments and User Comments (legend at the top). Has a green Print Report button.

Resolved Issues Report

Shows every issue that was Resolved within a From / To date range you pick (defaults to the last 30 days).

Output: grouped by Property, with each resolved issue showing heading, description, and — uniquely to this report — a Resolution: N days metric showing how long the issue took to close. Below each issue, every comment in the thread is listed with date and author prefix.

Friday Status Report

The weekly update report, sent to stakeholders every Friday. Two flavours:

  • Detailed Report — every comment on every issue. Comprehensive but long.
  • Summarized Report — only the most recent N comments per issue (pick 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 or 10). Good for busy recipients who want the headline without the full history.

Output: grouped by Status (Resolved → Unresolved → Issue), then by Property (country, then name). Each issue shows heading, description, resolution days (if resolved), the selected comments, and a "View all comments" link when the thread was truncated. Resolved items are filtered to only those resolved within the last 7 days OR with a comment added in the last 7 days — this keeps the report focused on recent activity rather than cluttering it with every historical closure. Unresolved and Issue items are always shown regardless of age. Has a teal Submit FSR button in the header to commit the report.

Which report when? Comments Report = "what did we talk about recently". Resolved Issues Report = "what did we close in this period + how fast". Friday Status Report = the weekly snapshot you send to stakeholders.
Best Practices
  • Keep Issue Headings short and specific — "Water Leak" is better than "There's a problem with water". They appear in every report and list; scannability matters.
  • Put context in Description, updates in Comments — Description is the initial statement of the problem and shouldn't change. Comments are where the story evolves.
  • Always add a closing comment when marking Resolved — explain what fixed it. Six months from now someone will thank you.
  • Just write the comment — prefix is automatic — date and author initials like (DM), (SS) get inserted by the system. Focus on clarity of content, not formatting.
  • Sort by Status (urgency) + Date Logged (oldest) — Unresolved issues that have been open for weeks are the ones to chase first.
  • Use "Issue" status sparingly — it's for genuine escalations, not just items that have been open a while. Flipping something to "Issue" is a signal that the current approach isn't working and attention is needed. Most things that stay in Unresolved for a long time are simply slow, not stuck.
  • Use Search liberally — when a tenant mentions "the thing with the balcony", search "balcony" across all properties to find the right thread fast.
  • Comments Report weekly, Friday Status Report weekly — different audiences. Internal team wants the comments firehose; stakeholders want the Friday snapshot.
  • Summarized FSR with 2–3 comments is the sweet spot for most recipients — enough context to understand, not so much they won't read it.
  • Resolution days red in the Resolved Issues Report is a self-audit — anything over 30 days is worth asking "why did this take so long?". Feeds process improvement.
  • Don't delete Resolved issues — the history is valuable and doesn't clutter the default view (filter to Unresolved if you want the current worklist).
Delete is permanent and takes every comment with it. Reserve it for true mistakes (duplicate issue, wrong property, test data). Don't use it to "clean up" old stuff — that's what filters are for.
What is the Projects page?

Projects is where you plan and track multi-step work on a property — renovations, refurbishments, larger coordinated repairs, anything that goes beyond a single Issue. Unlike Issues (which are discrete problems), a project is a hierarchy of tasks and subtasks with budgets, assignees, progress tracking, and start/end dates.

The hierarchy

Everything nests in three levels:

  • Project — the top-level container (e.g. "Refurbish Palikaridi Apartment"). Linked to exactly one property.
  • Task — a chunk of work within the project ("Update Kitchen", "Bedrooms Update"). Has a priority, status, dates, budget, assignee and progress %.
  • Subtask — a step within a task ("Backsplash", "Granite Top Replacement", "Paint Bedrooms"). Same fields as a task.
Auto-calculated project totals

The project itself doesn't carry dates, status or progress manually. They are rolled up from its tasks and subtasks:

  • Start Date = earliest start across all tasks
  • End Date = latest expected completion across all tasks
  • Progress % = weighted rollup from task-level progress percentages
  • Status = Pending / In Progress / Completed, derived from the task progress rollup

Change a subtask's progress, and the parent task and project both update automatically.

Superuser only: Add, Edit and Delete are Superuser actions. Regular Users can browse projects and view all the detail.
The eight columns

One row per project. Clicking the Project Name opens the full detail page.

Project Name

The name plus a truncated description underneath. The whole cell is clickable and links to the project detail page.

Property

Which property the project is for. A project is always linked to exactly one property.

Status (auto-calculated)

A coloured badge: Pending — no tasks started yet. In Progress — work underway. Completed — all tasks done.

Progress (auto-calculated)

A teal progress bar plus the weighted percentage. Rolled up from subtask progress through their parent tasks.

Start Date / End Date (auto-calculated)

Earliest task start and latest expected completion. Shows Not set in grey when there are no tasks yet.

Edit / Delete

Superuser-only buttons. Edit opens the project form (limited to name, property, description — dates/status/progress can't be edited directly). Delete is a red bin that permanently removes the project and all its tasks and subtasks.

Filters

Three filters — Search (name or description), Property, and Status. Search uses the teal icon button or press Enter; the other two auto-submit on change. Active filters show as removable tags below. Click any tag's × to drop just that filter, or use Clear All to reset everything. The panel auto-expands when any filter is active so you can see what's applied.

Adding a new project

Click Add New Project. The form is deliberately minimal:

Project Name *

Required. The display name — keep it specific ("Refurbish Palikaridi Apartment" beats "Palikaridi Work").

Property *

Required. Dropdown of all properties. Once set, the project is locked to this property.

Project Description

Optional free text. Shows as the secondary line under the project name on the main list.

No dates or status at creation — the blue callout on the Add form reminds you: project dates and status are derived from tasks, so there's nothing to set up front. Create the project, then add tasks and subtasks; the totals will populate themselves.
Editing a project

Opens a richer form with:

  • Language tabs: English / Greek — lets you store localised names/descriptions for multilingual Task List output
  • Name, Property and Description — editable
  • Start Date, Expected Completion, Status — shown but read-only and labelled "(Auto-calculated)". A blue note explains: to change these, edit the individual tasks.
Delete removes everything underneath: all tasks and subtasks go with the project. There's no undo — the confirmation dialog on delete is your only safety net.
The Project Detail page

The heart of the module. Clicking any project name opens it. Four top-right buttons: Task List, Gantt Chart, Duplicate Project, Back to Projects.

Four summary cards

At the top of the detail page, four cards show the rolled-up numbers:

  • Progress — weighted % with a small progress bar
  • Total Tasks — count of top-level tasks
  • Budgeted Cost — sum of all task and subtask budgets (€)
  • Actual Cost — sum of actuals so far (€)

Below the cards: Start Date, Expected Completion, Actual Completion (shows Not completed until the project finishes).

Project Tasks section

All tasks listed with their subtasks nested underneath. Each card shows:

  • MEDIUM / LOW / HIGH priority badge
  • Task name, description, + Add Subtask button, Status badge, Edit and Delete icons
  • Start / Expected End / Budgeted / Actual / Progress figures inline
  • Subtasks group showing completion count and aggregate progress (e.g. "0/2 completed - 21.9%"), collapsible

Subtasks show the same info as tasks, plus an Assigned field showing the team member responsible.

Editing a task or subtask

Clicking the pencil icon opens the task edit form. Fields:

  • Language tabs (English / Greek) for localised task name/description
  • Task Name *, Priority (Low/Medium/High), Status (Pending/In Progress/Completed)
  • Task Description
  • Start Date, Expected Completion Date
  • Budgeted Cost, Actual Cost
  • Assigned To, Progress Percentage (0–100)

The progress rule is enforced: Pending = 0%, In Progress = 1–99%, Completed = 100%. Sub-task edit also shows breadcrumb context at the top — Project, Property, Parent Task.

Duplicate Project

The Duplicate Project button on the detail page creates a copy of the project with all its tasks and subtasks. Useful for repeatable workflows like standard apartment refurbishments — clone the template, swap in the new property, adjust.

Two alternate views of a project

Beyond the card-based detail page, the project has two companion views.

Task List

Opens an assignee-friendly, printable task list. Before generating, you pick:

  • Assignee — All Team Members or a specific person (filters the output)
  • Language — English or Greek (uses the localised task names from the edit form's language tabs)

The generated list shows every task and subtask in a flat sortable table with: Task Name + description, Type badge (PROJECT / TASK / SUBTASK), Priority, Status, Start Date and Expected End.

Red dates with ⚠ flag overdue items. Summary line at the bottom gives total task count and completion rate. Print and Export Excel buttons sit top-right.

Gantt Chart

A visual timeline of the project and all its tasks/subtasks as horizontal bars across a calendar. Each bar is filled proportionally with its progress % and labelled with the number.

Controls: View Mode dropdown (daily / weekly / monthly), toggles to show Start Date and Duration columns, plus Zoom Out / Zoom In / Fit to Screen buttons.

Bars are clickable — click any bar to open the edit form for that task or subtask. Useful when spotting a late task on the timeline and jumping straight into updating it. Print button top-right for a hard-copy version.

Which view when? Task List is the "what needs doing" view for contractors and assignees — print it out, hand it over. Gantt Chart is the "how does this fit together over time" view for planning and status meetings.
Best Practices
  • Project = multi-step work, Issue = one thing to fix — don't raise an Issue for a full kitchen refurb; that's a Project with tasks.
  • Break tasks down until each one has a single owner and < 2 weeks of work — that's the sweet spot for meaningful progress tracking. Bigger chunks become "always at 30%" black holes.
  • Keep project dates empty on create — they're auto-calculated. Just add tasks and the dates appear.
  • Fill in actual cost as you go — the Budgeted vs Actual gap on the detail page is your cost-overrun early warning.
  • Set assignees on every task — unassigned tasks become nobody's problem. The Task List generator filters by assignee, so an empty assignee field means tasks will be invisible when Marko prints his list.
  • Use the Greek language tab for Cyprus properties — if your contractor works in Greek, add the Greek task names once and Task List output in Greek becomes a click.
  • Follow the progress rule strictly — Pending = 0%, In Progress = 1–99%, Completed = 100%. Don't leave a completed task at 95% because "there's a bit of cleanup left"; raise a new task for the cleanup.
  • Print the Task List for on-site work — contractors don't live in the app. Physical paper with the checklist works.
  • Duplicate standard projects — if you've nailed the task breakdown for a typical two-bedroom refurbishment, duplicate it next time and just change the property.
  • Gantt Chart for weekly review — the visual makes it obvious which tasks are slipping. Click the red-highlighted bars straight into the edit form.
  • Clean up finished projects? Don't — keep them as historical reference. Use the Status filter to show only In Progress when you want a current worklist.
Delete cascades: deleting a project removes every task and subtask beneath it, irreversibly. If you just want it out of your daily view, let it complete naturally and filter by Status instead.
What is the Actual Expenses page?

Actual Expenses is the running ledger of real money spent on properties — maintenance, repairs, supplies, professional fees, anything you actually pay out. Each row is one expense, tied to one property, with an optional scanned invoice or receipt attached.

Every expense passes through a two-step approval & payment lifecycle with email notifications at each step. Once an expense is both Approved and Paid, it flows through automatically to the P&L Actual view under the Financials module — matched to the year of the expense date.

Actual vs Budgeted
This page handles Actual expenses only — what was really spent. Budgeted expenses are configured separately in Financials → Configuration.
Who can do what
  • Anyone — add a new expense, upload or replace the invoice document (even on approved expenses)
  • Anyone (while Not Approved) — edit expense details, delete the expense
  • Superusers only — edit details or delete an expense after it's been Approved, and Approve or mark Paid
Three notification emails fire per expense: approval request (on add), approval confirmation (on approve), payment confirmation (on pay). Recipients are configured in Administration → Notification Settings.
The columns

One row per expense. Normally 7 columns. When this page is opened from Financials → P&L Actual, it shows a read-only 4-column view (Date, Property, Description, Amount only).

Date

The date the expense was incurred. This is the date used to match the expense to a P&L year.

Property

Which property the expense is for. A small green file icon appears next to the property name when an invoice document is attached — click it for a quick inline preview without opening the Manage modal.

Description

Free text (max 55 characters) — what the expense was for. Keep it specific ("ATOM sprayed for cockroaches", "Replacement of faucet in kitchen").

Amount

Expense amount in €, formatted with thousand separators and two decimals.

Manage

Opens the Manage Expense modal with three sub-tabs: Expense Details, Invoice Document, Delete Expense. Always visible to all users; the contents inside adapt to permissions.

Approved?

For Superusers: an Approve button while pending, flipping to a disabled Approved indicator once done. For regular users: read-only status (Approved / Pending).

Paid?

For Superusers: a Pay button (only enabled once the expense is Approved; before that it shows Approve First and is disabled). Flips to a disabled Paid indicator once paid.

The filter panel

Click the panel header to expand. The panel starts collapsed on every page load, but auto-expands whenever filters are active or just cleared — so you can always see what's applied.

Search Expense

Free-text search on the expense description. Click the teal icon button or press Enter to apply.

Property

Narrow to a single property. Auto-submits.

Status

Three lifecycle-based filters:

  • Require Approval — new expenses awaiting Superuser review. This is the approval queue.
  • Approved and Not Paid — the payment queue. Approved expenses that still need to be paid out.
  • Approved and Paid — closed. These feed P&L Actual.
From Date / To Date

Date-range filter. Filters by expense Date column. Useful for end-of-month reconciliation or matching expenses to a bank statement period.

Active filter tags & Clear All

Applied filters show as removable tags under the dropdowns. Click × on any tag to remove just that filter, or use Clear All to reset everything.

Adding a new expense

Click Add New (top-right). Anyone can add an expense — it's not superuser-restricted, because the approval workflow handles the controls.

Four required fields:

Date

Date picker (dd/mm/yyyy). Use the actual date the expense was incurred, not today. This date determines which P&L year the expense eventually counts toward.

Property Name

Dropdown of all properties. The expense is locked to this property once saved, so pick carefully.

Expense Description

Up to 55 characters. Aim for specific — "Mr Edison call out fee for extractor fan" beats "Repair work".

Amount

Numeric (two decimal places). Enter as a positive number.

No invoice upload at this stage

The Add form doesn't include file upload. After saving, open Manage → Invoice Document tab to attach the receipt or invoice PDF.

Saving triggers an email: the Expense Approval Request notification fires immediately to whoever is configured in Administration → Notification Settings. The expense starts life as Not Approved / Not Paid.
The Manage Expense modal

Click any Manage button in the table to open it. Three sub-tabs inside:

1. Expense Details

Edit form with Date, Property, Description, Amount. Access depends on approval status & role:

  • Not Approved / Not Paid — anyone can edit
  • Approved (paid or not) — regular users see a "Read Only" warning; only Superusers can still edit

Superusers additionally see two dropdowns inside this form:

  • Approved ? (Yes / No)
  • Paid ? (Yes / No) — smart-disabled when Approved = No (forced to No automatically, so you can't accidentally mark something Paid before approving it)
2. Invoice Document

Always accessible — everyone, at every status. Even if an expense is fully Approved and Paid, regular users can still upload, replace or manage the invoice.

When no document exists: Upload form accepting PDF, JPG, PNG, Excel, Word (max 5MB).

When a document exists: inline preview (PDFs inline, images rendered, other files with a placeholder) plus four buttons:

  • Download — grab the file
  • Add to Existing (PDF-only) — merges a new file into the existing PDF. All file types get converted to PDF first. Perfect for bundling the original invoice + bank transfer screenshot + contractor's quote into one evidence pack.
  • Replace — swap the existing document for a new one (discards the old)
  • Delete Document — removes the attached file (leaves the expense intact)
3. Delete Expense

Permanently deletes the expense and the attached document. The tab itself is hidden for users who don't have delete permission (regular users on Approved expenses).

Requires ticking "I understand this action cannot be undone" before the red delete button enables. Shows a summary of the expense (Date, Property, Description, Amount) for a sanity check before confirming.

Quick invoice view (from the table)

Click the green file icon next to a property name to open the Quick Invoice Viewer — a compact modal with just the document preview and a Download button. Skips the full Manage modal when you only want to glance at the receipt.

The end-to-end expense lifecycle

Every expense follows the same four-step flow, with email notifications firing at each transition.

Step 1 — Capture

A user (anyone) adds the expense via the Add New form. Saves as Not Approved, Not Paid.

Email fires: Expense Approval Request — goes to the TO/CC recipients configured in Administration → Notification Settings (typically the Superuser who approves).

Step 2 — Approve

Superuser reviews the expense (optionally the attached invoice) and clicks the blue Approve button on the row. Status flips to Approved, Not Paid.

Email fires: Expense Approved notification — lets the original user know their expense has been cleared, and notifies anyone configured in Notification Settings.

Step 3 — Pay

Once the actual payment goes out (bank transfer, cash, card — outside the system), the Superuser clicks the blue Pay button. Status flips to Approved, Paid. This button is disabled with "Approve First" until step 2 is done — the system won't let you mark Paid on an unapproved expense.

Email fires: Expense Paid notification — closes the loop for the originator and any configured recipients.

Step 4 — Flows to P&L Actual

Once the expense is both Approved AND Paid, it's automatically included in the Actual column of the Financials P&L for the year of the expense Date. No manual step — the P&L view always reflects all fully-processed expenses. (More detail on how P&L aggregates these lives in the Financials help modal.)

Why two steps instead of one? Approve and Pay are separated deliberately so authorisation (we agree this is a legitimate expense) happens before the money actually moves. The "Approved and Not Paid" filter is your accounts-payable queue.
Notification recipients are configured per notification type. If any of the three emails isn't arriving, check Administration → Notification Settings for the relevant type (Expense Approval Request, Expense Approved, Expense Paid) and confirm the TO/CC fields are filled.
Best Practices
  • Capture expenses fast — don't let receipts pile up. Photo of the receipt + same-day entry is the gold standard. Later entry risks forgetting the context.
  • Use the actual expense date, not today's — critical because the date determines which year's P&L the expense lands in. Backdate when necessary.
  • Always attach the invoice — via Manage → Invoice Document. An expense without an invoice is a reconciliation headache. It's also what the Superuser reviews before approving.
  • Keep descriptions specific — "Mr Edison call out fee for extractor fan" tells you everything when reviewing six months later. "Repair" doesn't.
  • Use "Add to Existing" for bundled evidence — scan the invoice, screenshot the bank transfer, photo the contractor's quote — merge them into one PDF on the expense record. One click away from a full audit trail.
  • Superuser weekly ritual: filter to "Require Approval" — clear that queue once a week. Don't let approvals drag; it delays payment and the originator's email confirmation.
  • Superuser monthly ritual: filter to "Approved and Not Paid" — this is your accounts-payable list. Pay them down, update the status here as each one clears.
  • Reconcile monthly against the bank statement — use the From/To Date filter to match the statement period and tick off each line. Any mismatch = something to investigate.
  • Approved & Paid items flow to P&L automatically — no need to manually copy numbers anywhere. Just trust the workflow.
  • Don't delete paid expenses to "clean up" — they're history. Delete is meant for mistakes (duplicates, wrong property, test entries), not tidying.
  • Cross-check annual totals against bank records — at year-end, the sum of Approved & Paid expenses in Status filter should match what actually left the bank.
Permanent delete cascades: deleting an expense removes the attached invoice document with it, irreversibly. If you only want to swap the file, use Replace on the Invoice Document tab — much safer.
