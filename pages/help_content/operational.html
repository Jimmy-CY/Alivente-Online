What is the Suppliers module?

The Suppliers module is your central directory of contractors, service providers and trade contacts who work on your properties. Whenever you need to call a plumber, an electrician, an air-conditioning technician or your local property manager — this is the place to find their details.

Typical suppliers you might track:

  • Handymen — general repairs and maintenance
  • Plumbers — water, leaks and bathroom work
  • Electricians — wiring, fittings, electrical issues
  • Air-conditioning technicians — servicing and repairs
  • Pool maintenance — cleaning and chemical balancing
  • Fumigators — pest control
  • Property managers — on-the-ground representatives
  • Builders / construction firms — larger structural work
Why keep this list?
Having vetted, ready-to-call suppliers per country and per trade saves time when something breaks. No hunting through old emails or text messages.
Add New Supplier

Click the Add New button (top-right) to add a supplier. You'll be asked for the following:

  • Contact Person — the individual you actually call (e.g. "Costa (Agia Thekla)")
  • Contact Number — phone number, ideally with country code
  • Email Address — optional, leave blank if none
  • Company Name — if they trade under one (otherwise leave as None)
  • Role / Service — what they do (Plumber, Electrician, Property Manager, etc.)
  • Country — Cyprus, Greece or Spain
Edit an Existing Supplier

In the supplier table, click Edit in the Details column for the supplier you want to update. The same form opens with the existing details pre-filled. Make your changes and click Save.

Delete a Supplier (superuser only)

The red trash icon in the Delete column appears only for superusers. Clicking it opens a confirmation popup showing the supplier's details (Contact Person, Company, Role, ID).

Permanent action: Deleting a supplier cannot be undone. All associated data is removed. If you're not sure, edit the entry instead.
Using the Filter Panel

The Supplier Filters panel sits above the table. Click the panel header to expand or collapse it. Two filters are available:

Search Contact Person

Type a name (or part of one) and press Enter or click the search button.

Example: typing Vasilis will match both "Vasilis" and "Vasilis Aggelatos".

Country

Filter by country (Cyprus, Greece or Spain). Selecting a country auto-submits the form. Choose All Countries to remove this filter.

Active Filter Tags

When filters are applied, they appear as removable tags below the filter form. Click the small × on any tag to remove that single filter.

Clear All

The Clear All button (top-right of the filter panel) resets every filter back to its default and reloads the full supplier list.

Combine filters: The two filters work together. For example, search Property Manager in country Cyprus to narrow the list to property managers in Cyprus only.
What each row button does
Details — Edit

Opens the supplier's record in edit mode. Use this to update phone numbers, change role, switch country or correct typos.

Report — View

Opens a clean, printable Supplier Details Report showing the supplier's full profile in three sections:

  • Contact Information — phone and email
  • Company Details — company name and role
  • Location — country
Delete (superuser only)

Opens the delete-confirmation popup. The supplier is only removed once you click Delete Permanently in the popup — clicking the trash icon alone does nothing destructive.

Best Practices
  • Use a recognisable contact name — if you know two people called "Costa," disambiguate them in the contact field (e.g. Costa (Agia Thekla)) so you can tell them apart at a glance.
  • Always include the contact number — an email-only supplier is hard to reach in a hurry. Include the country code where possible.
  • Be consistent with the Role / Service — pick a short label and stick with it (Plumber, not "Pluming Guy" or "Mr. Fix-It"). Consistent labels make filtering far more useful.
  • Set the Country accurately — suppliers don't usually travel between countries, so country becomes your most useful filter when an issue arises.
  • Edit, don't delete, when a supplier becomes inactive — consider keeping them with a note in the role (e.g. "Plumber — retired") instead of deleting, so historic records still make sense.
  • Use the Report view to share details — the printable report is handy when you need to forward supplier info to a tenant or property manager.
  • Cross-link with Issues — once a supplier is in this directory, they can be assigned to property issues so the right tradesperson is notified for the right job.
Can't see the Delete column? The Delete option only appears for users with superuser privileges. If you need to delete a supplier and don't see the column, ask an administrator.
What is the Invoices page?

This page shows every open (unpaid) invoice in the system — one row per outstanding invoice, with the tenant, the amount owed, and how many days overdue it is.

Once you mark an invoice as paid, it disappears from this list. So the page acts as a live worklist of money still to be collected.

How invoices get here
  • Automatically — the system generates a fresh invoice for every active tenant on the 1st of each month.
  • Manually — a Superuser can click Generate Invoices in Administration to create any missing invoices on the fly (for example, if the 1st-of-month run was missed, or a tenant was added mid-month).
Related screens: the Notifications Dashboard shows the count of overdue invoices at a glance, and Administration → Notification Settings controls who is emailed when an invoice is marked paid.
Superuser only: Regular users see an Access Restricted message on this page.
The eight columns

Each row is one open invoice. Columns left to right:

Property

The property this invoice is for.

Country

Country where the property is located (Cyprus, Greece, Spain, etc.).

Tenant

The tenant who owes the rent.

Amount

Monthly rent, displayed in € with no decimals (right-aligned, monospace for easy column scanning).

Invoice Date

When the invoice was generated. For automatic invoices this is normally the 1st of the month.

Due Date

When payment is due. Calculated from the invoice date plus the tenant's payment terms (which are set on the tenant's record).

Days Overdue

How many days past the due date this invoice is. Shown in red bold when greater than zero — a clear visual cue that the invoice needs chasing.

Mark as Paid

A "Paid" link — click it once payment has been received to settle the invoice. See the Mark as Paid tab for what happens next.

Filtering the list

The Invoice Filters panel sits above the table. Click the panel header to expand or collapse it. Two filters are available:

Property

Narrow the list to invoices for a single property. Auto-submits on change — you don't need to click anything else.

Tenant

Narrow the list to invoices for a single tenant. Useful when chasing one specific person/company across multiple properties (where applicable). Also auto-submits.

Active filter tags

When filters are applied, they appear as removable tags below the dropdowns. Click the × on any tag to remove that single filter and reload the list.

Clear All

The Clear All button (top-right of the filter panel) resets both filters and reloads the full list of open invoices.

Combine filters: Property and Tenant filters work together. Picking both shows only invoices that match both conditions.
Marking an invoice as paid

When you've confirmed that the tenant has paid, click the Paid link in the right-hand column. Three things happen:

1. The invoice is closed in the system

The invoice's status flips to Paid and it is recorded as settled in the financial records.

2. The row disappears from this list

This page only shows open invoices, so a paid invoice is removed from view immediately.

3. A notification email is sent

An Invoice Marked as Paid email is sent to whoever is configured in Administration → Notification Settings for that notification type. This typically goes to the person who tracks invoice payments and reconciles accounts.

Confirm payment first: Only click Paid after you've actually verified the money has landed in the bank. There's no easy "Unpaid" reversal from this page.
Best Practices
  • Make this a weekly check — open the page once a week. Anything in red is a phone call or email to make.
  • Red Days Overdue is the only thing that matters at a glance — sort your attention by that. Recent invoices (0 days overdue) are not a concern yet.
  • Pair this with the Notifications Dashboard — the Dashboard shows the overdue invoice count without you having to open this page. Use the Dashboard for awareness, this page for action.
  • Always verify payment before clicking Paid — check your bank statement, then mark. Marking a row as Paid removes it — reversing it is much harder than waiting until you're certain.
  • Use filters when chasing — if you're calling one tenant, filter by them so you can see all their open invoices in one go.
  • Empty list is a good thing — when this page is empty, every tenant is fully paid up. That's the goal.
  • Configure the "Invoice Marked as Paid" notification — in Administration → Notification Settings, set TO and CC recipients so the right people are kept in the loop when payments come in.
  • If the 1st-of-month auto-run didn't fire — head to Administration → Generate Invoices and click it. Safe to click any time; it only creates invoices that don't already exist.
Expecting an invoice that isn't appearing? Check (1) the tenant has an active lease covering this period, (2) the 1st-of-month auto-run fired (or click Generate Invoices in Administration), and (3) the tenant's monthly rent is set on their record.
What is Petty Cash?

Petty Cash is a simple running ledger of small cash transactions related to property management. It tracks every euro that goes in and out of your physical petty cash float — with a live closing balance at the bottom of the table.

Typical things you'd record here:

  • Income — opening balance, top-ups (cash added to the float by Alivente), reimbursements paid in cash
  • Expenses — small property-related purchases paid in cash: cleaning supplies, small repairs, tips, parking, minor materials, postage, etc.
Why use this? Larger expenses go through the Expenses module with proper approval workflows and invoices attached. Petty Cash is for the small day-to-day stuff that wouldn't justify the overhead — the kind of thing you'd otherwise scribble on a receipt and forget about.
The closing balance should match your physical cash: If you reconcile properly, the green/red number at the bottom of the table is exactly what you should be holding in the petty cash tin or envelope at any moment.
The four columns
Date

When the transaction happened (not necessarily when it was entered into the system).

Description

Free-text description of the transaction. Best practice is to prefix with the property name when applicable — for example "Apolloneon — Demetri: Carrying washing machine upstairs" or "Lykavitto: Princess cleaned storeroom". Non-property entries (Opening Balance, Alivente Top-Up) skip the prefix.

Amount

The value of the transaction. Color-coded:

  • Green — Income (cash coming in, positive number)
  • Red, prefixed with − — Expense (cash going out)
Income / Expense

A clear text label of which type the row is — redundant with the colour but improves scannability and works for users with colour-vision difficulty.

CLOSING BALANCE row

The bottom row of the table is always the cumulative running balance — every income added, every expense subtracted, from the very first row to the last.

  • Green closing balance = positive cash on hand (the normal state)
  • Red closing balance = negative balance (something's wrong — either a missed top-up or a data-entry mistake)
Adding a new transaction

Click Add New (top-right) to open the Add Transaction form. It's a single row of four fields:

Date

Date picker (dd/mm/yyyy). Pick the date the transaction actually happened, not today — especially important when entering older receipts late.

Transaction Description

Free text. Use the "Property: detail" convention so future-you can scan the ledger and instantly see which property each cost relates to.

Amount

Enter the amount as a positive number. Don't add a minus sign — the Income / Expense dropdown handles whether the amount is added or subtracted from the balance.

Income / Expense

Dropdown — pick Income for cash coming in (top-ups, opening balance) or Expense for cash going out. This determines the colour and sign in the table.

Click Save to commit the transaction. Back returns to the ledger without saving.

Common mistake: Don't enter -30.00 in the Amount field with Expense selected — the system will subtract the negative, effectively adding 30 to your balance. Always enter 30.00 as Amount and let the Expense type handle the minus.
Best Practices
  • Reconcile regularly — physically count the cash in the petty cash tin and compare to the closing balance on screen. They should match. If they don't, find the missing transaction.
  • Use the "Property: detail" description format — e.g. Lykavitto: Bought rubbish bags @ Green Tree. Makes audit and review trivial. Skip the prefix for non-property entries (Opening Balance, top-ups).
  • Be consistent with naming — pick one form for top-ups (e.g. "Alivente Top-Up") and stick with it. Inconsistent variants like "Alivente: Top Up", "Alivente Top-Up", "Alivente top up" make the ledger harder to read at a glance.
  • Always positive amounts — enter 30, not -30. The Income / Expense type does the work.
  • Enter as soon as possible — the longer between the cash event and the entry, the more likely you'll forget what it was for. Phone-photo of the receipt + same-day entry is the gold standard.
  • Keep the physical receipts — the digital ledger is your record but receipts are the backup. File them by month in case you ever need to evidence a transaction.
  • Top up before you run out — if the closing balance is getting low, request an Alivente Top-Up before you're stuck unable to pay for something small.
  • Larger spend → Expenses module — if a transaction is big enough to warrant approval, an invoice or a paper trail beyond a receipt, record it in the Expenses module instead of here.
Closing balance gone red? Either a top-up was missed or an expense was entered twice. Scan recent rows for duplicates first, then check whether a recent top-up made it in.
What is the Dashboard?

The Property Dashboard is a visual, property-centred hub. Pick one property from your portfolio and every relevant view of it — legal documents, financial performance, tenant, expenses, issues, valuation — is one click away.

Use it as the starting point when you want to investigate a single property end-to-end, rather than jumping in and out of module-by-module screens from the main menu.

What's on screen

The dashboard is organised into three sections, each grouping the cards that naturally belong together:

  • Property — physical and legal aspects (Property Report, Title Deed, Valuation, Issues)
  • Tenant — who lives there and lease status (Tenant Report, Lease, Invoices, Lease Renewals)
  • Financials — money flows and performance (Revenues, Actual Expenses, Budgeted Expenses, Profit & Loss)
Cards are dim until you pick a property. That's deliberate — there's nothing meaningful to show until the dashboard knows which property you want to look at.
Three steps to use the Dashboard
1. Click the property selector

A modal opens listing every property in your portfolio.

2. Pick a property from the list

The modal closes, the selector turns darker teal and shows your property name, and every card lights up — the dashboard is now armed for that property.

3. Click any module card

You're navigated to the relevant view for the selected property.

Switching property

Click the property selector again at any time to swap to a different property. All cards reflect the new selection immediately — no need to navigate away.

Deep linking

You can pre-select a property by adding ?property=ID to the dashboard URL. Useful when linking from other parts of the system.

Keyboard

Esc closes the property picker modal.

The Property section

The four physical and legal aspects of the property:

Property Report

A multi-section snapshot: Property Address, Specifications (m², year built), Utility Accounts, Insurance & Status, and an Asset Summary with statistics, financial summary, assets grouped by category, and a full Asset Details table with warranty expiry dates.

Title Deed

View-only. Displays the uploaded title deed PDF inline. To upload, replace or delete the deed itself, use the Properties module.

Valuation

Compares Purchase Price against Current Value, with the gain/loss in € and as a percentage, plus a green/red trend arrow.

Issues

Maintenance, repairs and any open issues for this property. When there are none, shows a green "Excellent! No issues have been reported" empty state.

The Tenant section

Everything about who's currently renting the property and the state of their lease:

Tenant Report

The current tenant: Type (Individual or Company), Contact Information, Lease Period (start/end dates), Financial Information (deposit, monthly rent, levies, payment terms), and Lease Details (rental type, renewal clause, notice period, renewal status, active flag).

Lease

View-only. Inline PDF display of the current lease agreement. Page title shows the property and tenant in parentheses for context.

Invoices

Open invoices for this property's current tenant. Header shows the Total Amount Due in red. Each row lists Invoice Date, Due Date, Days Overdue, Amount, and a red Overdue badge. Scoped to this property only — for the system-wide list, use the main Invoices module.

Lease Renewals

The renewal state of the current lease. While active, shows a green "Current lease is active. No renewal action required at this time." together with the tenant name and end date. As expiry approaches, the screen prompts for renewal action.

The Financials section

Money in, money out, and the resulting performance:

Revenues

Breakdown of the property's revenue lines — typically Rental and Levies — with Revenue Type (Monthly, Yearly) and Amount. Pulled from the tenant's lease data.

Actual Expenses

A table of expenses actually incurred, with Date, Description, Amount, and a green invoice icon that opens the scanned receipt PDF. A Year filter sits in the top-right; the bottom row sums to Total Actual Expenses for [year].

Budgeted Expenses

The planned expense schedule. Each row: an Expense Line Type (Communal Fees, Company Tax, Insurance, Property Manager, etc.), an Expense Type indicating when (a specific month like June, or Monthly), and the budgeted Amount. This is the baseline that Actual is compared against.

Profit & Loss

A full 12-month P&L statement: Revenue rows, Expenses rows, Net Profit — monthly breakdown plus Total and Monthly-average columns. A Budget toggle switches between Actual and Budget views. Below the table, a Financial Indicators panel surfaces Gross ROI, Net ROI, Expenses to Revenue, Rent €/m² and % Value Increase.

Why split Expenses into Actual and Budgeted? Comparing the two is the heart of expense control. Looking at either alone only tells half the story.
Best Practices
  • Always pick a property first — everything else is locked until you do. The dim cards are a deliberate hint.
  • Use this as your starting point per property — if you're investigating one specific property end-to-end, the Dashboard is faster than going module-by-module from the sidebar.
  • Compare Actual vs Budgeted Expenses — click both in turn to spot variances. Big swings either way are worth investigating.
  • Switch properties without leaving the page — click the selector at the top. Faster than going Back and re-entering.
  • Use deep links — bookmark ?property=ID URLs for properties you check often.
  • Profit & Loss is the executive view — the full 12-month grid plus the Financial Indicators row gives a property's complete financial picture in one screen. If you only have time for one card per property, it's this one.
  • Use the Budget toggle on Profit & Loss — flip between Actual and Budget to see over- and under-performance against the plan.
Cards still dim after picking a property? Try re-opening the picker and selecting again. If the issue persists, refresh the page.
